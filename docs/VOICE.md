# High Society voice

How High Society sounds in its own copy, and the rules writers follow so that new pages sound like the same business.
Facts come from `facts/hs-facts.json`; this file covers voice only.

**Basis.** High Society's own prose on the 30 site pages of the v1 capture (highsocietydispo.com, 2026-09-16) and the
v2 homepage (`v2-source/src/content/home.json`): 491 sentences after removing customer reviews, navigation, turn-by-turn
directions and the hidden template block from another business. Customer reviews are the customers' voice, not
High Society's, and are not counted.

## In short

High Society writes like a friendly, polished store host. It is warm and service-first, uses plain retail language, and
mixes short, upbeat marketing lines with practical, step-by-step help. It talks to "you" and speaks as "we" and "our
team". It leans on a small set of positive words (premium, quality, curated, exceptional, elevated, convenient) and never
uses stoner slang, hype words or health claims.

## Tone

- **Hospitable and reassuring.** The customer is looked after: staff are trained, knowledgeable and there to help, and
  first-timers are welcome.
- **Positive, not loud.** Marketing lines praise quality and service with mild adjectives. Exclamation marks are rare:
  15 of 491 sentences (3%), mostly upbeat FAQ answers and calls to action.
- **Practical.** How-to copy (first visit, pickup orders, directions) is numbered or stepwise, one action per sentence.
- **Respectful of the rules.** Age, ID and responsible use are stated plainly and often, without lecturing.

## How it addresses the customer

- Second person: 146 of 491 sentences (30%) speak to "you/your".
- First person plural: 150 of 491 (31%) speak as "we/our/us"; staff are "our team", "budtenders", "staff", "our security
  and front desk".
- The reader is a "customer" on store pages, a "guest" in hospitality copy (About, Careers), a "first-time visitor" or
  "returning customer" in how-to copy, and a "member" in loyalty copy.

## Sentence length and shape

- Mean 13 words, median 12; nine in ten sentences are 22 words or fewer; the longest is 36.
- Paragraphs run two to four sentences. FAQ answers are one to three sentences and open with the answer: "Yes", "No",
  "Absolutely!".
- Lists are short noun phrases ("Curbside Pickup", "In-Store Pickup", "In-Store Shopping").

Phrases High Society repeats: "premium cannabis products" (29 mentions), "customer service" (16), "amazing deals",
"Elevating the cannabis experience", "Join The Society", "Download the High Society App", "Order Now",
"whether you're new or ...", "Our team is happy to help".

Words and registers High Society never uses (0 mentions in its own copy, checked by regex over all 30 pages): world-class,
revolutionary, game-changer, ultimate, epic, insane, mind-blowing, life-changing, cure, heal, treat, wellness, stoned,
blazed, fire, dank, vibes, 420, folks, "Pure Michigan", mitten, Great Lakes.

## What it emphasises

1. **Hospitality and service.** Trained, knowledgeable staff who ask the right questions.
2. **Quality.** Premium products from trusted vendors that passed Michigan testing.
3. **Value.** Deals, the loyalty program and app deals.
4. **Convenience.** Online ordering for pickup, curbside, the app, quick visits.
5. **Community.** Engaging local communities through events and partnerships.
6. **Michigan.** The hero line, Michigan testing standards, and each store's town and nearby attractions.

## Rules for writers

1. **Name.** Call the business "High Society" in prose (PLAN decision 7). Not "High Society Dispo", "HS", "High Society
   Cannabis" or "Pit Stop" (Pit Stop is the visual brand system, not a name in copy). The loyalty program is "The
   Society"; its call to action is "Join The Society". Name stores by town, as High Society does: "our Big Rapids store",
   "High Society Big Rapids".
2. **Person.** Speak to the reader as "you"; speak for the business as "we", "our team", "our budtenders".
3. **Length.** Keep most sentences between 8 and 20 words and none over 35. Paragraphs of two to four sentences. FAQ
   answers start with the answer ("Yes.", "No.") and add one or two sentences.
4. **Word choice.** "Cannabis" in prose. "Weed" only where a page title or H1 needs the keyword, as High Society's own
   titles do. "Marijuana" only in legal and medical terms ("medical marijuana card"); statute names keep their own
   spelling.
5. **No hype.** Use High Society's own mild praise words sparingly (one or two per paragraph). Never use the words in the
   "never uses" list above. Do not add superlatives as facts ("best", "leading", "largest", "#1", "top"): High Society's
   existing claims stay on its existing pages; new pages do not add more.
6. **No health claims.** High Society never says cannabis cures, heals or treats anything; keep it that way. Describe
   effects modestly and never as a medical outcome (PLAN acceptance criterion 4: no cure claims, nothing that appeals to
   minors, 21+).
7. **Michigan-proud, factually.** Tie the copy to Michigan the way High Society does: its stores, their towns and the
   attractions its pages name, and Michigan's testing standards. Do not invent local partnerships, events, sponsorships
   or history; the only founding fact is 2020. Do not use "Pure Michigan".
8. **21+ and responsible use, in High Society's words.** Every page carries a 21+ line. Use these phrasings:

- "21+ only. Please consume responsibly." (/dispensary-locations/new-buffalo/service-areas/)
- "Valid government ID required. 21+ only. Selection varies—check today's availability." (/dispensary-locations/new-buffalo/service-areas/)
- "Recreational shopping is available to individuals 21 years or older." (/dispensary-locations/big-rapids/)
- "Individuals 18 and older with a medical marijuana card can also make purchases." (/dispensary-locations/big-rapids/)
- "Enjoy our cannabis products responsibly." (/dispensary-locations/big-rapids/)
- "Use cannabis responsibly." (/dispensary-locations/birch-run/)

   For travel, use High Society's own line: "don’t travel across state lines with cannabis".

9. **Facts only from `facts/hs-facts.json`.** Prices, deals, brands and ratings are never stated as current; point to the
   menu or the app. Where High Society publishes nothing (see `notPublished`), write neutrally ("ask your store", "check
   the menu") and log the gap in `docs/OPEN-DECISIONS.md`.
10. **Exclamation marks.** At most one per section, in a call to action or an upbeat FAQ answer. Never "!!!".
11. **Do not imitate High Society's errors.** No typos copied from its pages ("Rapdis", "comming", "Craftmanship", "Says",
    "early early"); no "infamous" as praise; no city boilerplate repeated across stores ("this vibrant city ...
    something for everyone"); write each store's copy for that store.
12. **Reviews.** Quote a customer review whole, with the name as shown, or not at all. Never edit or invent one.
13. **Residue rule.** No competitor names, brands, stores or blog names in High Society copy, even in comparisons.
