Michigan Medical Marijuana Reciprocity: A Visitor's Guide

Visiting Michigan with another state's medical card? Here's what Michigan law allows, what to bring, how the limits work and why your cannabis has to stay in Michigan.

Key takeaways

  • Michigan gives a valid out-of-state medical card the same force as a Michigan card.
  • Visiting patients buy at licensed provisioning centers with an unexpired card and photo ID, within Michigan's medical limits.
  • Visitors 21 and older can skip the card question and shop adult-use with government-issued photo ID.
  • Nothing bought in Michigan can legally go home to Indiana, Ohio, Illinois or Wisconsin.
  • Call your High Society store before using an out-of-state card.

Michigan medical marijuana reciprocity is real. Michigan law honors valid medical cards from other states, and licensed medical provisioning centers may sell to visiting patients who show an unexpired card and photo ID. If you're 21 or older, there's an even simpler path: shop adult-use with your government-issued ID. Either way, what you buy has to stay in Michigan. Here's what visitors should know.

What Michigan medical marijuana reciprocity means

Michigan's medical marijuana law gives a visiting patient's registry card from another state the same force and effect as a Michigan card. State rules then let licensed provisioning centers sell to visiting patients, as long as the store checks a valid, unexpired card and photo ID and applies Michigan's medical purchase limits.

You can't get a Michigan card as a visitor, though. Those go only to Michigan residents, who can learn how to apply in our medical card guide.

Possession protection vs. buying at a provisioning center

Those are two different protections:

  • Possession. With your valid card and photo ID, you're treated like a Michigan patient, who may carry up to 2.5 ounces of usable marijuana.
  • Buying. Purchases happen at licensed medical provisioning centers. They count as medical sales, so you skip Michigan's 10% adult-use excise tax and pay only the 6% sales tax.

Michigan patients are protected from arrest only when they show a valid registry card with photo ID, so carry both.

At High Society, adult-use shopping is for customers 21 and older. Valid government IDs from other states are accepted in most cases, so bring yours and our team will check it. Medical patients 18 and older with a valid card are welcome too, but call your store before counting on an out-of-state card.

What to bring

  • Photo ID issued by a government, such as a driver's license or passport. Michigan stores check ID before every sale.
  • If you're buying as a patient, your state's medical card, unexpired.
  • A plan for the drive: keep purchases sealed and out of reach, and never use cannabis while driving.

Medical purchases are checked against those daily and monthly limits in Michigan's statewide monitoring system. See our Michigan cannabis laws guide for possession limits and our Michigan cannabis FAQ for shopping questions.

Going home: why cannabis can't cross state lines

Whatever you buy in Michigan stays in Michigan. Michigan's protections stop at the state line, and no federal law allows carrying cannabis across it, even for patients. As of October 2026, the neighbors add their own risks:

  • Indiana has no medical or adult-use program, and possessing any amount is a crime.
  • Ohio allows possession only of cannabis bought from an Ohio-licensed dispensary or legally grown at home.
  • Illinois says cannabis shouldn't be transported across state lines.
  • Wisconsin makes possession a crime.

As our New Buffalo pages put it: enjoy responsibly in Michigan, and don't travel across state lines with cannabis. Our guide to using a medical card in other states covers the neighbors in more depth.

Digital cards, expired cards and other edge cases

  • Expired card. It doesn't count. Michigan requires a valid, unexpired card, so shop adult-use instead if you're 21 or older.
  • Digital or printed card. Michigan accepts another state's card or its equivalent, but the store still has to check it, so call ahead and ask which formats work.
  • Paperwork that isn't a card. An application receipt or a doctor's form may not be enough. In Michigan's own program, neither counts as a card.
  • Under 21 without a card. You can't buy cannabis in Michigan.

Before you visit: call your store, or order ahead

If you plan to use an out-of-state medical card, call the store first and ask whether it can serve visiting patients. Store details are on each location page, like our New Buffalo dispensary page.

Shopping adult-use? Order ahead online for in-store pickup, or choose curbside and we'll text you when your order is ready. 21+ only. Please consume responsibly.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use my Indiana or Ohio medical card in Michigan?

Yes for Ohio, if your card is valid and unexpired. Michigan law gives another state's card the same force as a Michigan card, and licensed provisioning centers may sell to you with photo ID. Indiana doesn't issue medical cards, so Hoosiers 21 and older shop adult-use instead. Call ahead to make sure your store serves visiting patients.

Do I need to register before visiting?

No. Michigan doesn't ask visiting patients to register in advance. You show your valid, unexpired card from home and a photo ID at a licensed medical provisioning center, which checks them and applies Michigan's medical limits. Michigan cards themselves are only for Michigan residents, so visitors can't apply for one.

Can visitors under 21 buy cannabis in Michigan?

Only with a valid medical card. Adult-use sales are for adults 21 and older, so visitors 18 to 20 need a valid, unexpired card from home and a photo ID to buy at a licensed medical provisioning center, within Michigan's medical limits. In Michigan's own program, patients under 18 rely on a parent or guardian as caregiver. High Society welcomes medical patients 18 and older, but call ahead about out-of-state cards.

Can I take Michigan cannabis home to another state?

No. Michigan's protections end at the state line, and federal law gives no permission to carry cannabis across it, even into another legal state. Indiana and Wisconsin make possession a crime, Ohio allows only cannabis from its own licensed dispensaries or legal home grows, and Illinois says cannabis shouldn't cross state lines. Enjoy it responsibly in Michigan.
