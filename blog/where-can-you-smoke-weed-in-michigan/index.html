Where You Can (and Can't) Legally Consume Cannabis in Michigan

The short answer is private property with permission. Here's what Michigan law says about public places, rentals, lounges, cars, parks, federal land and campuses.

By the High Society teamPublished October 24, 20266 min read

06min readNews & Culture
In this article

  2. What counts as a public place, and the fines
  3. Rentals, apartments and hotels: what landlords may ban
  4. Licensed consumption venues and how cities opt in
  5. Cars and boats: transport, passengers and impaired driving
  6. Parks, beaches and federal land
  7. College campuses and federal-funding rules
  8. Legal ideas for a great smoke spot at home
  9. Frequently asked questions

Key takeaways

  • Michigan's adult-use law doesn't allow cannabis use in public places; private property where you have permission is the safe default.
  • Landlords and hotels can ban smoking, but a lease can't stop a tenant from using cannabis in ways other than smoking.
  • Lounges exist only where local government has authorized consumption areas, and they must be closed to anyone under 21.
  • No one may use cannabis while driving or operating a boat, and no one may smoke in a car's passenger area on a public road.
  • Federal law applies on federal land, and universities ban cannabis on campus under federal funding rules, so leave it at home.

Where can you smoke weed in Michigan? The short answer: on private property, with permission from whoever owns or manages it. Michigan's adult-use law doesn't cover using cannabis in public places, while driving or on school grounds, and federal land follows federal law. Here's a plain-language rundown for adults 21 and older. It's general information, not legal advice.

Where can you smoke weed in Michigan? Private property first

Your own home is the simplest answer. A friend's home works too, as long as they're fine with it, since whoever owns, occupies or manages a property can ban smoking or use there. If you rent, check your lease first.

What counts as a public place, and the fines

The law doesn't allow using cannabis in a public place. The one exception is an area a city, village or township has designated for consumption that isn't open to anyone under 21.

Places open to the public are the clear cases: sidewalks, streets, parks, beaches, parking lots, bars and restaurants. Cannabis isn't allowed at all on preschool and K-12 school grounds, on school buses or at correctional facilities.

Using cannabis where the law doesn't allow it, while staying within the possession limits, is generally a civil infraction with a fine of up to $100, and you can lose the cannabis. Violations involving driving or school grounds are handled more seriously. For possession limits and the rest of the state's requirements, see our Michigan cannabis laws guide.

Rentals, apartments and hotels: what landlords may ban

Landlords can ban or limit cannabis on their property, and hotels and other businesses can prohibit consumption on their premises. There's one important limit: a lease can't stop a tenant from lawfully possessing cannabis and consuming it by means other than smoking.

So a no-smoking lease doesn't necessarily bar an edible at home. Vaping is a gray area, so read your lease closely and ask your landlord if you're unsure. Our guide to discreet cannabis use covers low-odor options, and our cannabis weekend getaways guide helps you find cannabis-friendly stays.

Licensed consumption venues and how cities opt in

Each Michigan city, village or township decides whether to allow cannabis businesses at all. Local governments can also authorize consumption in designated areas closed to anyone under 21, plus limited special events. Where they've said yes, you may find a consumption lounge; check its policies before you go. Our explainer on what a cannabis lounge is covers how they work.

Cars and boats: transport, passengers and impaired driving

No one may use cannabis while driving or in control of a car, boat, snowmobile, off-road vehicle or aircraft. Smoking in the passenger area of a vehicle on a public road isn't allowed either, and that includes passengers.

Adults can carry up to 2.5 ounces in Michigan. The cautious way to transport it is sealed and out of reach, ideally in the trunk. Michigan law bars driving while under the influence, so plan a sober ride before you start, not after.

Parks, beaches and federal land

State and local parks and public beaches are open to the public, so the public-use limit applies there, and parks may post their own policies too.

Federal land is stricter. Michigan's law doesn't protect you in places like Sleeping Bear Dunes or a national forest, because federal law applies there and, as of October 2026, adult-use marijuana is still a Schedule I drug federally. Leave cannabis at home when you visit. Our guide to cannabis tourism destinations can help you plan trips around these limits.

College campuses and federal-funding rules

Colleges that receive federal aid must follow federal drug-free campus laws, so cannabis stays off campus, even for adults 21 and older. Here's how three Michigan universities put it:

  • Michigan State University's 2018 guidance bars using or possessing marijuana on MSU property or during MSU business or events, citing federal law.
  • Ferris State University's 2018 policy statement prohibits marijuana in any form, including medical, on university-owned or leased property, citing its federal aid.
  • Central Michigan University's drug-free campus policy, last revised in 2016, prohibits illicit drugs on university property and at university activities.

If you live, work or visit near these campuses, keep cannabis off university property.

Legal ideas for a great smoke spot at home

Home is where the law and your comfort line up. A few ideas:

  • Pick the right spot. A porch, patio or yard you own or have permission to use, or a well-ventilated room.
  • Be a good neighbor. Keep smoke out of shared hallways, or go smoke-free with edibles in shared buildings.
  • Store it safely. Keep cannabis locked up and away from kids and pets. At home, anything over 2.5 ounces must be kept locked by law.
  • Keep it 21+. Invite only adults 21 and older, and plan rides for guests.

Pick up what you need at any of High Society's eight Michigan stores, then head home to enjoy it. Our FAQ answers common questions about shopping with us. Please consume responsibly, and don't travel across state lines with cannabis.

Frequently asked questions

Is it legal to smoke weed in public in Michigan?

No. Michigan's adult-use law doesn't allow cannabis use in public places, apart from consumption areas a local government has designated and closed to anyone under 21. Using cannabis in public while within the possession limits is generally a civil infraction with a fine of up to $100, and you can lose the cannabis.

Can I smoke weed in a Michigan state park?

No. State parks and public beaches are open to the public, so the public-use limit applies, and parks can post their own policies too. National parks, lakeshores and forests are federal land, where federal law applies and adult-use marijuana is still illegal. Enjoy the park, and save the cannabis for home.

Can my landlord ban cannabis in Michigan?

Partly. Landlords can ban or limit smoking and other cannabis activity on their property. But under Michigan's adult-use law, a lease can't stop a tenant from lawfully possessing cannabis or using it by means other than smoking, such as edibles. Read your lease closely, especially any language about vaping.

Can a passenger smoke weed in a car in Michigan?

No. Michigan's adult-use law doesn't allow anyone to smoke cannabis in the passenger area of a vehicle on a public road, passengers included. Drivers can't use cannabis at all while in control of a vehicle. Keep cannabis sealed and out of reach, ideally in the trunk, and wait until you're home.
