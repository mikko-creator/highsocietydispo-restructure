Strong Stuff: High-Potency Flower, Concentrates, Edibles, Vapes and Drinks

For experienced adults: how potency is measured, what counts as strong in each category from flower to drinks, and how to enjoy stronger products without overdoing it.

Key takeaways

  • High-potency products are for experienced adults with an established tolerance; newcomers should wait.
  • Flower and concentrates usually list THC as a percentage, while edibles and drinks usually list milligrams.
  • In Michigan, adult-use edibles are capped at 10 mg of THC per serving and 200 mg per package, and drinks at 10 mg per serving.
  • Research links higher-potency cannabis with greater risks of psychosis and cannabis use disorder.
  • Take less than usual with any stronger product, wait before more, and never drive after using cannabis.

High-potency cannabis products pack more THC into each puff, bite or sip, and they're made for experienced adults. If you've built a tolerance and want something stronger, this guide covers the strong options in each category. It also explains how to read potency on a label and how to stay in control.

Newer to cannabis? Begin with lower-potency products and save this guide for later.

Who high-potency cannabis products are for, and who should wait

Stronger products suit adults with an established tolerance who already know how a format affects them. Others should hold off, including:

  • Anyone new to cannabis, or new to a format like dabs or edibles.
  • Anyone who has had a rough reaction to THC, like panic or a racing heart.
  • People who are pregnant or breastfeeding.
  • People with a personal or family history of psychosis.
  • Anyone taking medications who hasn't talked with a clinician about cannabis.

There's a reason for the caution. A 2022 systematic review of 20 studies found that higher-potency cannabis was associated with a greater risk of psychosis and cannabis use disorder. Results for depression and anxiety were mixed.

NIDA also notes that anxiety and distrust are more common with large amounts and strong products. If you have questions about your health and cannabis, talk with a clinician.

How potency is measured: percent vs. milligrams

Potency is usually shown in two ways, depending on the product:

  • Percentage for flower and concentrates: how much of the product's weight is THC, usually shown as total THC including THCA. Do the math and a gram of flower at 20% carries some 200 mg of THC, though not all of it reaches you.
  • Milligrams for edibles, drinks, tinctures and capsules: the THC in one serving and in the whole package.

On high THC products sold by the milligram, Michigan sets firm limits:

ProductAdult-use limitMedical limit
Edibles10 mg per serving, 200 mg per package50 mg per dose, 200 mg per package
Tinctures and capsules10 mg per dose, 200 mg per package100 mg per dose, 2,000 mg per package
Drinks10 mg per serving, 100 mg per container10 mg per serving, 100 mg per container

A high percentage doesn't tell the whole story, as our guide to what counts as high THC explains.

Flower: what counts as high THC, and why terpenes matter too

There's no official line for high-THC flower. For a reference point, a large analysis of Washington's legal market from 2014 to 2016 found flower averaged about 21% THC. NIDA notes that flower and concentrates in dispensaries can exceed 40%.

THC isn't the only thing that shapes a session. Two jars at the same percentage can feel quite different. Terpenes, the aroma compounds in each strain, may play a part, though research on them is early, and dose, tolerance and setting matter too. Many people find flavor-forward flower a more satisfying upgrade than chasing the highest number. Our list of high-THC strains covers well-known names and why their numbers vary.

Concentrates: textures and strength

Potent concentrates gather a plant's cannabinoids and terpenes into a small, strong package, and they're the strongest category by percentage. In the same Washington data, extracts averaged 68.7% THC, more than triple the average for flower.

They come in many textures: glassy shatter, soft wax and budder, crumbly concentrates, runny sauce and crystalline diamonds. Live resin and live rosin start from fresh-frozen plants to keep more of their aroma. NIDA warns that dabbing can deliver a large amount of THC very quickly. Start with a portion about the size of a grain of rice. Our concentrates guide explains each type.

Infused pre-rolls: flower plus concentrate or kief

Infused pre-rolls add concentrate, such as oil or rosin, or a coating of kief to flower. That raises the THC in each pre-roll well above what the flower alone carries.

Try a puff or two, then pause before deciding on more. They're also easier to enjoy when shared. Our guide to infused pre-rolls covers the common styles.

Strong edibles and why slow onset makes them tricky

Strong edibles can catch even experienced people off guard, and timing is why. Swallowed THC can take 30 to 90 minutes to start, peaks at 2 to 3 hours and lasts about 4 to 12 hours. Taking another piece before the first one peaks, often called stacking, is the classic mistake.

In Michigan, adult-use edibles top out at 10 mg of THC per serving and 200 mg per package. Registered medical patients can buy edibles of up to 50 mg per dose. Even experienced consumers do well to have one serving and wait at least 2 hours.

High THC vapes: fast, strong, easy to overdo

High THC vapes, especially concentrate cartridges, put a lot of THC into each puff. Inhaled THC is felt within minutes and peaks in about 15 to 30 minutes.

The convenience is the risk: a vape doesn't burn down the way a joint does, so it's easy to keep going. Take one puff, wait 15 minutes, then decide. Stick with licensed, tested cartridges.

Potent cannabis drinks for experienced drinkers

Potent cannabis drinks are still limited in Michigan to 10 mg of THC per serving and 100 mg per container, though a multipack can hold more in total. Some cans hold a single serving and others hold several, so read the label before you open one.

Sip slowly, give it time before reaching for a second and keep alcohol out of the mix. Drinks and edibles share one advantage: measured servings make your dose easier to control than with flower or a vape.

Staying in control: tolerance, spacing doses and tolerance breaks

  • Start below your usual amount with any stronger product, even in a format you know.
  • Space your doses: about 15 to 30 minutes between puffs, and at least 2 hours between edible servings.
  • Mind your tolerance. A brain-imaging study looked at 11 men who used cannabis heavily. Their cannabinoid receptor availability was about 15% lower than in a comparison group, and the gap closed within 2 days of stopping. A tolerance break can reset how strongly you feel THC, and your old amount may hit harder afterward.
  • Know the signs of too much and what to do; our guide to coming down comfortably covers both.
  • Never drive after using cannabis, and store strong products locked away from kids and pets.

Seasonal note: fall and winter nights in

Michigan's long fall and winter nights invite evenings at home, and some people save hard hitting cannabis for them. If that's your plan, pick a night with nowhere to go, especially when roads are snowy. During holiday visits, lock away strong edibles and concentrates, and label anything infused. A flavor-forward option you can dose in small steps often makes a better evening than the strongest thing you can find.

Frequently asked questions

What counts as high potency for flower?

There's no official cutoff. For context, flower in Washington's legal market averaged about 21% THC in one large analysis of 2014 to 2016 sales, so flower well above that is strong by most standards. THC percentage is only one piece of the experience, though; terpenes, tolerance and how much you use matter too.

Do infused pre-rolls hit harder than plain ones?

Usually, yes. Adding concentrate or kief raises the THC in each pre-roll above what the flower alone carries. In one large market study, concentrates averaged more than triple the THC of flower. Try a puff or two, wait about 15 minutes and share rather than finishing one alone.

What is the strongest edible dose sold in Michigan?

For adult-use buyers, edibles are capped at 10 mg of THC per serving and 200 mg per package. Registered medical patients can buy edibles with up to 50 mg per dose and 200 mg per package. Tinctures and capsules for patients can go higher, up to 100 mg per dose.

How do I avoid overdoing a strong product?

Start below your usual amount, then wait about 15 to 30 minutes for inhaled cannabis and at least 2 hours for edibles before having more. Skip alcohol, stay somewhere comfortable and plan on no driving. Our THC dosage guide walks through dosing step by step.

What are the strongest cannabis products?

Concentrates, by THC percentage. In one large analysis of Washington's legal market from 2014 to 2016, extracts averaged 68.7% THC compared with about 21% for flower. NIDA warns that dabbing delivers a lot of THC fast, which raises the risk of side effects. Percentage isn't everything, though: an edible's effects can last far longer.
