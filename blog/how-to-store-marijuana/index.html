Cannabis Storage Guide: Containers, Conditions and Child-Safe Habits

Heat, humidity, light and air wear cannabis down. Here's how to store flower, edibles and concentrates so they keep, and how to keep them away from kids and pets.

Key takeaways

  • Store cannabis airtight, cool, dark and at steady humidity, roughly 55 to 65 percent for flower.
  • Glass jars beat plastic bags, and humidity packs help flower hold steady.
  • Keep edibles in their original child-resistant packaging, clearly labeled and out of reach.
  • THC slowly breaks down into CBN, faster with light and warmth.
  • In Michigan, anything over 2.5 ounces kept at home must be locked up.

Knowing how to store weed properly protects what you paid for: the aroma, the flavor and the potency. Cannabis breaks down with heat, humidity, light and air, so the goal is to keep it airtight, cool, dark and at a steady humidity. Storage is also about safety, especially if children or pets share your home. Here's how to handle flower, edibles and concentrates.

How to store weed: know what breaks it down

Four things wear cannabis down, and they often work together:

  • Light speeds up the breakdown of THC. In a four-year study of stored cannabis, light changed both how fast THC turned into CBN and how much CBN formed.
  • Heat speeds the same reaction. In that study, temperature changed how quickly THC broke down.
  • Air brings oxygen, which drives that slow conversion, and it lets aromatic terpenes escape.
  • Humidity cuts both ways. Too much invites mold, while too little leaves buds brittle and harsh.

How much difference does storage make? In the same study, cannabis kept at room temperature, in light or in the dark, lost almost all of its THC over four years. Samples kept dark in a refrigerator or freezer didn't show the same significant changes.

Does weed go bad, then? Over time it loses aroma and potency, and old flower turns dry and harsh. Mold is a different matter: if you see fuzz or smell mustiness, throw it out.

Best containers: glass, humidity packs and the trouble with plastic

The best everyday container is an airtight glass jar.

  • Glass doesn't hold odors, and a good seal keeps air and smell in. Tinted glass helps block light, though a dark cupboard does the same job.
  • Right-sized jars matter, because a mostly empty jar traps more air with your flower.
  • Humidity packs are small two-way packets that release or absorb moisture to hold a set level. An ASTM standard for dried flower sets a water-activity range of 0.55 to 0.65, roughly 55 to 65 percent relative humidity in a sealed container. It also notes that humidity-control packets can keep flower in that range.

Plastic is the weak spot. Thin bags let air and odor through and crush buds, and static can pull resin off the surface. Plastic tubs do better than bags but often aren't fully airtight, so once you open flower that came in plastic, consider moving it to glass. Our guide to cannabis accessories covers jars, cases and other storage gear.

Smell-proof and child-resistant options

  • Gasket-sealed jars are the simplest smell-proof option at home.
  • Smell-proof cases and bags use tight seals, and some add carbon linings, to keep odor in when you're carrying products.
  • Child-resistant packaging is required for edibles in Michigan, which must come in opaque, child-resistant packages. Keeping products in the packaging they came in is the easiest way to keep that protection.
  • Lockboxes add security for anyone with children or visitors at home, and they're an easy way to meet Michigan's locked-storage requirement for larger amounts.

Storing flower

For flower, aim for airtight, cool, dark and steady:

  1. Move flower into an airtight glass jar sized to the amount you have.
  2. Add a two-way humidity pack if you'll keep it for more than a few weeks.
  3. Keep the jar in a cool, dark cupboard or drawer, away from the stove, sunny windows and heating vents.
  4. Open it only when you need some, and grind just before use, since ground flower loses aroma faster.
  5. Label jars with the product name and purchase date so you use older flower first.

Our cannabis flower guide covers how to judge freshness at the counter.

Storing edibles, clearly labeled

Edibles need the same care as any food, plus extra caution, because they can look like ordinary snacks.

  • Keep them in their original child-resistant packaging, which also shows the THC per serving.
  • Store chocolate somewhere cool and dry, keep gummies sealed so they don't dry out, and chill homemade baked goods in a clearly marked container.
  • Keep edibles apart from everyday snacks and out of children's reach, ideally locked.
  • Label homemade edibles with what they contain and the date you made them.

This matters. U.S. poison centers recorded 207 edible cannabis exposures in children under six in 2017 and 3,054 in 2021. Nearly all of them, 97.7%, happened in a residence, and 22.7% of the children were admitted to a hospital. Our cannabis chocolate guide has more on infused chocolate.

Storing concentrates and vape carts

  • Concentrates: keep them in small, airtight glass or silicone containers, or wrapped in parchment inside a sealed container, somewhere cool and dark. Warmth can soften some concentrates, and air and light dull their aroma.
  • Concentrates you won't use for a while: some people refrigerate them. If you do, keep the container sealed and let it warm to room temperature before opening, so moisture doesn't condense inside.
  • Vape cartridges: store them upright at room temperature, away from heat and direct sun, with the cap on if they have one. Don't leave carts or batteries in a hot car.

Our vape care guide covers clogs and leaks.

Long-term storage

If you're keeping cannabis for months, cold and dark slow the breakdown most. In the four-year study, refrigerated and frozen samples held up far better than room-temperature ones.

Freezing works best as a one-time move for sealed product you won't touch for a while:

  • Use an airtight, freezer-safe glass jar or a vacuum-sealed bag, filled close to the top.
  • Freeze it once and leave it alone. Resin glands get brittle in the cold and can break off if you handle frozen flower.
  • Let the sealed container come to room temperature before you open it, so moisture doesn't condense on the flower.
  • Label everything with the date, and use older stock first.

Mistakes to avoid: oversized jars, plastic bags, the fridge and the freezer

  • Oversized jars: extra air speeds the loss of aroma and potency.
  • Plastic bags: they let air and smell through, crush buds and can strip resin.
  • The fridge for everyday flower: opening and closing the door brings temperature swings and condensation, which can encourage mold, and food smells can mingle with your stash.
  • The freezer for your daily jar: handling frozen flower knocks off brittle resin glands, and repeated thawing adds moisture.
  • Hot spots: cars, windowsills and the cabinet above the stove all run too warm.
  • Mixed storage: edibles next to regular snacks are an accident waiting to happen.

Safety at home: kids, pets and Michigan's locked-storage law

Michigan law lets adults 21 and older keep up to 10 ounces at home, plus whatever their own plants produce. Anything over 2.5 ounces must be stored in a container or area with locks or other functioning security devices. Our guide to Michigan cannabis laws covers the rest of the state's requirements.

Whatever the amount, a few habits keep everyone safe:

  • Store all cannabis up high, out of sight and, ideally, locked.
  • Keep products in their original packaging.
  • Never leave edibles, vape pens or concentrates out on counters, nightstands or in bags children can reach.
  • Remember pets, too. They can get into edibles and flower, so keep storage closed and off the floor.

If a child swallows cannabis, call the National Poison Control Center at 1-800-222-1222, the number printed on Michigan cannabis packages, or call 911 if symptoms are severe. If a pet eats cannabis, call your veterinarian right away. 21+ only. Please consume responsibly.

Frequently asked questions

How long does weed stay fresh?

Kept airtight, cool and dark at steady humidity, flower holds its quality for months, though the aroma fades first. Potency drops more slowly as THC converts to CBN, and light and warmth speed that up. In a four-year study, samples kept at room temperature lost nearly all their THC, while refrigerated or frozen samples held up far better.

Should you keep weed in the fridge?

Usually not for everyday flower. Opening and closing the door brings humidity swings and condensation, which can encourage mold, and food smells can mingle with your stash. Cold does slow THC breakdown, so sealed, long-term storage in a freezer can work if you freeze once and let the container reach room temperature before opening it.

What's the best container for cannabis?

An airtight glass jar sized to what you're storing. Glass doesn't hold odors, a good seal keeps air and smell in, and a jar without much empty space limits air exposure. Add a two-way humidity pack for flower and keep the jar somewhere dark. If children live with you or visit, a lockable box adds protection.

How should I store edibles?

Keep them in their original child-resistant packaging, which shows the THC per serving, and store them like the food they are: chocolate cool and dry, gummies sealed, baked goods chilled in a marked container. Keep edibles apart from everyday snacks and locked away if children live with you or visit. Label homemade edibles clearly.

Does cannabis lose potency over time?

Yes. THC slowly breaks down into CBN, and light, heat and air speed up the process. Aroma fades too as terpenes evaporate. Older flower mostly turns weaker and harsher, but anything showing mold should be thrown away. Storing it airtight, cool and dark slows the decline.
