THCV, the 'Diet Weed' Cannabinoid: What It Is and What's Known

THCV gets called 'diet weed,' but the human research is small and early. Here's what it is, how it acts in the body, how to spot it on a label and what the law says.

Key takeaways

  • THCV (tetrahydrocannabivarin) is a close chemical cousin of THC with a shorter side chain.
  • At low doses it tends to block the CB1 receptor behind THC's high; at higher doses in animal studies it can activate it.
  • Human studies on appetite and blood sugar are small pilots, so they don't show that THCV products cause weight loss or manage diabetes.
  • Michigan labels must list THC and CBD but not THCV, so look for THCV on the label or in lab results, and expect any product that lists THC to act like THC.

What is THCV? THCV, short for tetrahydrocannabivarin, is a minor cannabinoid that's chemically close to THC but behaves differently in the body. Online it's nicknamed "diet weed," because animal studies pointed to effects on appetite and blood sugar. The human research is much thinner: a few small studies with early, mixed results. Here's what THCV is, what scientists have tested, how it compares with THC, and how to spot it on a Michigan label.

What is THCV? THC's shorter-chain cousin

THCV shares THC's ring-shaped core, but its side chain is shorter: three carbon atoms instead of five. That small difference changes how it fits the body's cannabinoid receptors. The plant builds THCV through its own chemical pathway, and it's usually present in much smaller amounts than THC.

Because the names look alike, THCV often gets lumped in with THC. A 2025 review argued that's a mistake, since the two act differently at the doses studied in people. The review was written by researchers who work in the cannabinoid industry, so weigh it with that in mind. For background on where THCV fits among the rest, see our guide to cannabinoids.

What studies explore: appetite, blood sugar and THC's side effects

Most claims about THCV effects come from cell and animal research. Only a handful of small human studies exist, and they used pure, measured THCV rather than cannabis products.

Appetite

Animal studies suggested THCV might curb appetite, which is where the "diet" nickname and most THCV appetite claims started. The human evidence doesn't back that up yet. In a small brain-imaging study, 20 healthy adults took a single 10 mg dose of THCV or a placebo on separate days. During the scans they saw or tasted chocolate, and saw moldy strawberries or tasted an unpleasant strawberry flavor. THCV changed brain responses to both, but people rated the foods no differently. Weight-loss claims for THCV products rest on thin evidence: small, short studies rather than large clinical trials.

Blood sugar

In a 2016 pilot study, 62 adults with type 2 diabetes who weren't using insulin took THCV, CBD, one of two CBD-THCV mixes or a placebo for 13 weeks. THCV at 5 mg twice a day lowered fasting blood sugar compared with placebo and improved one measure of insulin-making cells. It didn't change HDL cholesterol, the study's main target. That's an early signal from one small trial, not a basis for managing blood sugar. If you have diabetes, keep your care plan with your clinician.

THC's side effects

A 2016 pilot gave 10 men, all occasional cannabis users, 10 mg of THCV daily for five days before an intravenous dose of THC. THCV felt no different from a placebo. It blunted the faster heartbeat THC usually causes, and nine of the ten said THC felt weaker. It also increased one kind of memory error, and the authors cautioned that the sample was tiny.

How THCV acts at CB1 and CB2, depending on dose

THCV's dual personality is what makes it interesting to researchers. A widely cited 2008 review of lab and animal work found that:

  • At CB1, the receptor behind THC's high, THCV blocks compounds that switch the receptor on.
  • At higher doses in animal studies, THCV can switch CB1 on instead, acting more like THC.
  • At CB2, found mostly on immune cells, THCV acts as a partial activator in lab tests.

In plain terms, a little THCV tends to push against THC's main action, while very large amounts may nudge in the same direction. How that plays out in people at everyday product doses hasn't been mapped. Our guide to the endocannabinoid system explains how these receptors work.

Does THCV get you high?

Not at the low doses tested so far. In the small studies above, 10 mg of pure THCV felt like a placebo. The 2025 review also describes a dose-ranging study of a related form, delta-8 THCV. Doses from 12.5 to 50 mg produced no THC-like effects, and 100 to 200 mg produced only very slight ones.

There's an important catch for shoppers. A product that features THCV may also contain THC, and THC brings its usual high. If the label lists THC, expect THC's effects and start low. So "non-intoxicating" describes pure THCV at low doses, not every product with THCV on the label.

Finding THCV: genetics, lab results and ratio products

THCV has grown popular across North American markets, but you can't spot it by sight or smell. Three clues help:

  1. Genetics. A plant's genetics set how much THCV it can make, and levels still vary from batch to batch. A strain name alone won't tell you how much a product holds, so people searching for THCV strains should look at test numbers instead.
  2. The label and lab results. Michigan requires every label to list THC and CBD, so THCV shows up only when the producer adds it, usually in milligrams per serving or as a percentage. Some producers also share batch lab results that break out minor cannabinoids.
  3. Ratio products. Gummies, vapes and tinctures built around THCV often state a ratio, such as THCV to THC, on the package.

If a product lists no THCV, don't assume it contains any. Questions about a label are easy to ask in person at your nearest High Society store, where selection varies by location.

Is THCV legal? Michigan, federal law and the 2026 hemp change

In Michigan's licensed market, THCV appears in marijuana products sold to adults 21+ and to registered patients 18+ with a valid card. Michigan counts cannabis with more than 0.3% THC, including THCA, as marijuana that only licensed businesses may sell.

At the federal level, adult-use marijuana remains a Schedule I drug as of October 2026. Separately, a federal change to the definition of hemp is set to take effect in late 2026, as of October 2026, and further changes are possible. It would end legal-hemp status for most intoxicating hemp-derived products. Our guide to the different types of THC follows how these rules touch hemp-derived products.

THCV and drug tests

Standard drug screens are built to find THC's breakdown products. If a THCV product also contains THC, a test can come back positive, just as it would after any THC product. Whether pure THCV on its own could trigger a positive hasn't been well studied. If you're subject to testing, count any product that lists THC as a THC product, and remember that detection windows depend on how often you use.

THCV is for adults 21+ or registered patients 18+. Please consume responsibly, and talk with your doctor before using any cannabinoid for a health reason.

Frequently asked questions

Can THCV cause a high?

Not at the low doses tested so far. In small studies, 10 mg of pure THCV felt no different from a placebo, and only very large doses of a related form, delta-8 THCV, produced faint THC-like effects. A product that features THCV may also contain THC, though, and THC produces its usual high. Check the label for both numbers and start low.

Is THCV really an appetite suppressant?

That isn't established in people. The idea comes mostly from animal research. In a small brain-imaging study of 20 healthy adults, a single 10 mg dose changed brain responses to food sights and tastes, but people rated the foods no differently. Larger, longer human trials would be needed before anyone could fairly call THCV an appetite suppressant.

How do I know whether a product contains THCV?

Read the label and any lab results. Michigan labels must show THC and CBD, so THCV appears only when the producer lists it, usually in milligrams per serving or as a percentage. Ratio products often state it up front, such as a THCV-to-THC ratio. If nothing lists THCV, don't assume it's in there, and ask at your store if you're unsure.

Could THCV trigger a positive drug test?

It could. If a THCV product also contains THC, standard tests that look for THC's breakdown products can come back positive, just as they would after any THC product. Whether pure THCV alone would trigger a test hasn't been well studied. If you're subject to testing, count any product that lists THC on its label as a THC product.

How is THCV different from delta-8?

They're different compounds. Delta-8 THC is a form of THC with one chemical bond in a different spot, and like delta-9 it activates the CB1 receptor, producing a high. THCV has a shorter side chain and, at low doses, tends to block that receptor instead. To add to the confusion, THCV itself comes in delta-9 and delta-8 forms.
