Weekend Getaway Ideas With Cannabis: From Cabin Trips to Staycations

Six weekend plans, from a cabin trip to a backyard hangout, paired with cannabis formats that fit, plus the Michigan travel basics that keep the trip legal.

Key takeaways

  • Adults 21+ can carry up to 2.5 ounces in Michigan, but public consumption isn't allowed and hosts can restrict cannabis on their property.
  • Keep cannabis sealed and out of reach in the car, never use it on the road, and never take it across state lines.
  • Federal land, including national forests and Sleeping Bear Dunes, falls under federal law, so leave cannabis out of those plans.
  • Stock up on the way: New Buffalo for Harbor Country, Mount Pleasant or Big Rapids heading north.

A good cannabis weekend getaway starts with two questions: where will you consume, and how will you get there and back? In Michigan, adults 21 and over can carry up to 2.5 ounces, but public consumption isn't allowed, property hosts can say no, and cannabis stays off-limits behind the wheel. Here are six Michigan weekend trip ideas, the formats that suit each, and the travel basics that keep it legal.

Planning a cannabis weekend getaway

Four questions do most of the work:

  • Where? On private property, with the host's OK. Not a beach, trail, park or car.
  • When? After the driving is done for the day.
  • What? Products that travel well in their original sealed packaging, like pre-rolls in tubes, vape cartridges and packaged edibles.
  • How much? Less than at home, since a long travel day and new surroundings can change how cannabis feels.

Pack only what you'll use; Michigan's carry limit is 2.5 ounces, no more than 15 grams of it concentrate. For more inspiration, see cannabis tourism destinations.

A cozy weekend in a rented cabin

A cabin Up North, or a cottage near the lake, is a classic Michigan weekend. Before you pack cannabis, ask the host: Michigan law lets property owners and managers ban or limit cannabis on their property, though a lease can't block a tenant from keeping it or using it any way but smoking. A listing that says nothing isn't a yes.

Asking about weed-friendly cabins in Michigan

Never assume a rental allows cannabis; get the answer in writing. If smoking is out but other use is fine, edibles or a tincture keep the cabin smoke-free. Bring a board game, and plan meals before anyone takes an edible.

An arts-and-culture weekend close to home

A staycation can feel like a real trip. Spend the day at galleries or a show and keep cannabis for the evening at home. Our East Lansing store's page lists the Eli and Edythe Broad Art Museum and the Wharton Center for Performing Arts as neighbors. The Charlotte page names the Courthouse Square Museum and the Charlotte Performing Arts Center.

Cannabis staycation ideas for the evening: a sketchbook session, a vinyl night or a film you've been saving, with a low-dose edible or a shared pre-roll. Never drive after.

A spa night at home

Some weekends don't need a road trip at all. A spa night at home pairs well with low, slow cannabis:

  • A face mask, a foot soak and a robe.
  • A cannabis topical, chosen for its scent and feel.
  • A low-dose edible or tincture taken early, so it peaks while you're settled in.
  • A calm playlist and your phone on silent.

Keep water close, and stick to one product at a time.

Hosting friends in the backyard

Your own backyard, or a host's with their blessing, is private property and a natural fit.

  • Keep it to adults 21 and over. Michigan law lets adults share as much as 2.5 ounces (concentrate capped at 15 grams) with other adults 21+ when nothing is received in return, and never with anyone younger.
  • Set out food, water and a low-dose option for lighter users.
  • Keep smoke and noise neighbor-friendly.
  • Line up rides home or spare beds early, and go easy if alcohol is on the table too.

A day outdoors, and the places you can't consume

Color tours, dune hikes and lake days define a Michigan weekend, but cannabis belongs back at your lodging. Michigan doesn't allow consuming cannabis in public places, so count beaches, parks and public trails as off-limits. On federal land, such as a national forest or Sleeping Bear Dunes National Lakeshore, Michigan's law offers no protection: federal law applies, and as of October 2026, possessing adult-use marijuana there is a federal offense. Michigan law also bars using cannabis while operating a motorboat, snowmobile or off-road vehicle.

More on this: cannabis and the great outdoors and where you can legally consume in Michigan.

A weekend alone to reset

Solo weekends are a good time to keep things simple. Choose a product you already know, take a little less than usual, and fill the days with unhurried things: a long morning walk, a real meal, a book or a journal. If you're somewhere remote, let someone know your plans.

Packing and travel laws in Michigan

These cannabis road trip tips keep the whole drive legal:

  • Possession: adults 21+ can carry up to 2.5 ounces, no more than 15 grams of it concentrate.
  • In the car: transporting cannabis is legal, but using it while driving isn't, and neither is smoking in the passenger area on a public road. The cautious move is to keep it sealed, out of reach, and in the trunk if you have one.
  • Driving: Michigan bans driving under the influence, so plan cannabis for after you've parked for the day.
  • State lines: carrying cannabis from one state into another breaks federal law, even between two legal states. Indiana, just over the line from our New Buffalo store, has no legal adult-use or medical marijuana as of October 2026.
  • Flying: TSA refers illegal substances it finds to law enforcement, and as of October 2026, adult-use marijuana is still federally illegal. Leave it home.

Frequently asked questions

Can I bring cannabis to a cabin rental in Michigan?

You can bring up to 2.5 ounces if you're 21 or older, but whether you can use it there is usually the host's call. Michigan law lets property owners and managers ban or limit cannabis on their property, though no lease may forbid a tenant from possessing it or from consuming it without smoking. Ask before you book, get the answer in writing, and keep products in their original packaging.

Can you use cannabis while camping in Michigan?

Plan on no. Michigan doesn't allow consuming cannabis in public places, so count public campgrounds, beaches and trails as off-limits. On federal land, such as a national forest, federal law applies, and possessing adult-use marijuana is a federal offense. On private land the landowner decides; otherwise, save cannabis for a private rental where the host allows it.

Can I drive with cannabis in my car in Michigan?

Yes. Adults 21 and over can transport up to 2.5 ounces, but you can't use it while driving or smoke it in the passenger area on a public road, and Michigan bans driving under the influence. The cautious approach: keep it sealed in the package it came in, stowed where nobody in the car can reach it, ideally the trunk.

Where can I buy cannabis near Michigan's lake towns?

Our New Buffalo store sits close to the Lake Michigan shore and Harbor Country, near I-94's first exit in Michigan, with free on-site parking. Heading north instead? Our Mount Pleasant and Big Rapids stores make good mid-Michigan stops. Check each store's page for hours, and don't travel across state lines with cannabis.
