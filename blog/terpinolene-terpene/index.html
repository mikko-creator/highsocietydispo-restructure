Terpinolene: The Fresh, Piney Terpene With a Citrus Edge

Terpinolene smells like pine with a citrus edge, and it leads the terpene list in only a minority of cannabis. Here is what it is, what research shows and how to find it.

The terpinolene terpene smells like pine with a sweet, citrusy edge, and it leads the terpene list in only a minority of cannabis. It's common in plants from tea tree to nutmeg, and it has an uplifting reputation in cannabis circles, yet mice that inhaled it became more sedated. Here's what's known, what isn't and how to find terpinolene-forward flower.

What is the terpinolene terpene?

Terpinolene is a monoterpene, a small, volatile aroma compound made by many plants, cannabis among them. Like pinene and ocimene, it belongs to the lighter terpenes that shape a strain's top notes.

Outside cannabis, it's a working ingredient. Manufacturers use it as a flavoring and as a fragrance in perfumes and soaps. For terpene basics, see our cannabis terpenes primer.

Terpinolene smell and taste: pine, sweetness and citrus

Chemical references describe terpinolene's aroma as sweet and piney, and its taste as somewhat sweet with a citrus note. The flavor industry files it simply under pine.

In cannabis, it stands out. A 2025 study had a panel smell 91 cannabis samples and found terpinolene was the one terpene consistently linked to specific aroma notes: citrus, and a sharper note the panel called chemical. The group of samples rated highest for those notes carried nearly eight times the average terpinolene level.

Where terpinolene occurs in nature

  • Trees. Many plants give it off, trees especially, and it is part of both pine oil and tea tree oil.
  • Citrus. It is a component of orange, mandarin, tangerine and bergamot oils.
  • Spices and herbs. It turns up in allspice and in the essential oils of nutmeg, mint and juniper. Parsnip oil is an unusually rich source.
  • Fruit and tea. It has been found in mangoes and in the tea plant. In a 2026 study of two mango varieties, terpinolene was the main aroma compound in one of them.

Some herbs in the mint family carry it too. Researchers who studied its effects in mice first isolated it from the leaves of one such herb.

What lab and animal research suggests, and its limits

Terpinolene research is early, and most of it happens outside the human body:

  • Animal work. In mouse studies, inhaled terpinolene had a sedative effect, and the effect came from the compound being absorbed into the body through the nose, not from its smell.
  • Lab work. Chemical databases note antifungal activity for terpinolene in lab tests.

The limits are big. Mice aren't people, the doses and the way they were given don't match a puff of cannabis, and terpinolene in flower comes mixed with cannabinoids and dozens of other compounds. None of this shows a health effect in humans.

Reported feel in cannabis: an uplifting reputation vs. sedation findings

In cannabis circles, terpinolene-rich strains have an energetic, uplifting reputation. Part of that may come from labels. A 2022 analysis of terpene data for about 43,000 US flower samples found a telling pattern: the terpinolene-rich group carried about twice as many sativa-labeled products as the overall mix would predict. It also carried about half as many indica-labeled ones.

The mouse research points the other way, since inhaled terpinolene made mice more sedated. Neither the reputation nor the mouse finding tells you how a terpinolene-rich strain will feel to you. THC level, dose and your own body matter more, so keep notes and judge by experience.

Why terpinolene-led strains are uncommon

Terpinolene-forward flower is in the minority. In that 2022 analysis, products sorted into three main chemical groups. About six in ten fell into a group led by caryophyllene and limonene, and the terpinolene-rich group was one of the smaller slices.

Researchers have mapped how often it shows up better than why. One clue is that the terpinolene pattern clusters in a small set of cultivar names: in a 2017 California study of 30 cultivars, the terpinolene group held just a handful of them.

Strains often reported as terpinolene-forward

Lab data does name a few. In the 2017 California study, the terpinolene-dominant group included Jack Herer and Trainwreck, with Ace of Spades and Sage assigned to the same class. Those samples also carried smaller amounts of compounds such as alpha-phellandrene and 3-carene. In the 2022 US analysis, 85.9% of products sold as Dutch Treat landed in the terpinolene-rich group.

These are examples from published datasets, not a list of what any store carries, and the same name from a different grower can test differently. Use them as clues, then check the batch's terpene panel.

Reading the terpene panel: is terpinolene in the top three?

Terpene results don't always appear on the package, so look for a terpene panel on the label, the menu listing or the producer's lab report. Then:

  • Check the top three. If terpinolene is first or second, expect its piney, citrusy character to come through. In third place, it's a supporting note.
  • Look for its usual companions. Terpinolene-rich flower often lists myrcene nearby, and sometimes alpha-phellandrene or 3-carene.
  • Trust your nose. A sharp, pine-and-citrus smell is a good hint.

Our guide to reading cannabis labels explains the rest of the package. The idea that terpenes shape effects alongside THC, known as the entourage effect, is still being studied. Questions in person are welcome at any of our eight Michigan stores, where our budtenders can help you compare terpene information. Cannabis is for adults 21 and older, or 18 and older with a medical marijuana card.

Frequently asked questions

Which aromas point to terpinolene?

Pine first, with a sweet, citrusy edge. Chemical references describe its smell as sweet and piney and its taste as lightly sweet and citrusy. In a 2025 study of 91 cannabis samples, terpinolene was the one terpene consistently tied to citrus notes, plus a sharper note the panel labeled chemical. If a jar smells like pine needles and lemon peel, terpinolene may be near the top.

Is terpinolene energizing or calming?

No one can say for sure yet. Terpinolene-rich strains carry an uplifting reputation and are more often sold under sativa labels, but mice that inhaled terpinolene became more sedated. Neither finding predicts how a strain will feel to you, because THC level, dose and your own body matter more. Start low and keep notes on what you notice.

Which strains tend to lead with terpinolene?

Published lab data points to names like Jack Herer and Trainwreck in one dataset and Dutch Treat in another, all with terpinolene-heavy profiles. Growers vary, though, so the same name can test differently from one batch to the next, and these are examples rather than a list of what any store carries. Check the terpene panel to be sure.

How rare are terpinolene-dominant strains?

They're in the minority. In a 2022 analysis of terpene data for about 43,000 US flower samples, about six in ten products fell into a caryophyllene-and-limonene group, and the terpinolene-rich group was one of the smaller slices. Terpinolene-led flower does turn up, but you'll usually need to look for it on purpose, using the terpene panel as your guide.
