Veterans and Cannabis: VA Rules, Michigan Access and the Research

A Veterans Day guide for those who served: what the VA allows, the federal limits that still apply, Michigan's medical program and what PTSD research has found.

By the High Society teamPublished October 24, 20267 min read

07min readCannabis 101
In this article
  1. Why veterans and cannabis belong in the Veterans Day conversation
  2. Veterans in cannabis advocacy
  3. VA medical marijuana policy: what VA allows
  4. Federal limits: VA grounds, federal jobs and firearms
  5. Michigan medical card and PTSD
  6. Cannabis and PTSD research: mixed and early
  7. Support for veterans
  8. Frequently asked questions

Key takeaways

  • VA clinicians can discuss cannabis with you but can't recommend it or complete state program forms, according to VA guidance as of October 2026.
  • VA says veterans won't be denied VA benefits because of marijuana use, but possession is prohibited on all VA grounds.
  • Michigan's medical marijuana program lists PTSD as a qualifying condition, yet research so far doesn't show cannabis is an effective PTSD therapy.
  • If you still serve, including in the Guard or Reserve, military policy applies regardless of Michigan law, and it bars hemp products such as CBD.
  • In a crisis, contact the Veterans Crisis Line: dial 988 and press 1, or text 838255.

Veterans Day is a good moment for an honest look at veterans and cannabis. Many veterans have practical questions. Will cannabis affect VA benefits? Can a VA doctor help with a Michigan medical card? What does research say about PTSD? This guide covers VA policy, federal limits, Michigan's program and where to find support. If you're in crisis right now, call the Veterans Crisis Line: dial 988, then press 1.

Why veterans and cannabis belong in the Veterans Day conversation

Cannabis use among veterans has grown. According to VA's National Center for PTSD, 9.8% of U.S. veterans reported using cannabis in the past year in 2019. Some use it to try to relieve PTSD symptoms, and several states specifically approve medical cannabis for PTSD. Clear, accurate information is one way to honor that service. This guide is educational, not medical advice.

Veterans in cannabis advocacy

Veterans have been among the strongest voices asking for real research. The first randomized, placebo-controlled trial of smoked cannabis for PTSD, published in 2021, enrolled military veterans. Its authors wrote that demand for that research had largely been driven by veteran advocacy groups.

Michigan has put money behind that research, too. Through the state's Veteran Marijuana Research grant program, Wayne State University received a five-year, $7 million award in 2021 to study cannabinoids, PTSD and depressive symptoms in veterans. Studies like this take years, and they're built to answer questions, not to promise results.

VA medical marijuana policy: what VA allows

VA's guidance, set out in VHA Directive 1315, comes down to four points:

  • You can talk about it. VA encourages you to discuss cannabis use with your providers, who will note it in your VA medical record.
  • Your benefits aren't at stake. VA says veterans won't be denied VA benefits because of marijuana use, and joining a state program doesn't affect eligibility for VA care.
  • VA won't recommend it or sign forms. VA clinicians may not recommend medical marijuana or complete state program paperwork.
  • VA won't provide or pay for it. VA pharmacies can't fill medical marijuana prescriptions, and VA won't pay for it from any source.

VA's guidance page was last updated in August 2023, and it still read this way in October 2026. That's despite an April 2026 federal regulation that moved marijuana covered by a state medical license into Schedule III, so ask your VA care team whether anything has changed.

Federal limits: VA grounds, federal jobs and firearms

Michigan law doesn't follow you everywhere.

  • VA grounds. Using or possessing marijuana is prohibited at all VA medical centers, locations and grounds, because federal law is in force there.
  • Federal jobs. Veterans who work for VA are subject to drug testing. If you work for another federal agency, check its drug-free workplace policy.
  • Firearms. Federal law restricts gun possession by people who unlawfully use controlled substances, and federal law still lists cannabis as one. In a narrow June 2026 ruling, the Supreme Court held that prosecuting one regular marijuana user under that law violated the Second Amendment. The law itself still stands as of October 2026, so if you have a firearm or plan to buy one, ask a lawyer how it applies to you.
  • Still serving. Active-duty, Guard and Reserve members follow military policy, whatever Michigan allows. Defense Department policy bars service members, including the Reserve Components, from using products that contain hemp, CBD included. Check with your command before using any cannabis or hemp product.

Michigan medical card and PTSD

Michigan's medical marijuana program lists PTSD among its qualifying conditions, alongside conditions such as chronic pain and cancer. If you're weighing a Michigan medical card for PTSD, remember that VA clinicians can't complete state program paperwork, so plan on working with a physician outside VA. Our step-by-step card page explains the application, and our patient guide covers life as a cardholder. To see what a card changes, read about Michigan medical card benefits.

A card doesn't change federal law or VA's policies, and it isn't a recommendation. Whether cannabis fits your situation is a conversation for you and your clinicians. For the wider legal picture, our Michigan law guide lays out the state's rules.

Cannabis and PTSD research: mixed and early

So far, the evidence is limited and mostly not encouraging:

  • The trial. In the 2021 study, 80 veterans with PTSD smoked one of three cannabis preparations or a placebo for three weeks. Every group improved, and no cannabis version beat placebo.
  • Reviews. VA's National Center for PTSD says research to date does not support cannabis as an effective PTSD therapy. It adds that chronic use might worsen trauma-related symptoms over time.
  • Clinical guidance. The 2023 VA/DoD PTSD guideline strongly recommends against using cannabis or cannabis derivatives for PTSD, citing a lack of evidence, known side effects and risks.
  • Risks. In one national study, about 12% of veterans with PTSD had a current cannabis use disorder. In another, cannabis use at the start of PTSD treatment was linked to double the risk of dropping out.

For people living with both PTSD and a substance use problem, VA's research summary points to a meta-analysis of four trials. Trauma-focused therapies worked better than non-trauma-focused ones, whether or not people used cannabis. For a wider look at the research, see cannabis benefits and risks. Please don't stop or swap a prescribed medication on your own; talk with your clinician first.

Support for veterans

You don't have to sort any of this out alone.

  • Veterans Crisis Line: dial 988 and press 1, text 838255 or chat online, confidentially.
  • Your VA care team: VA says talking about cannabis use won't cost you benefits.
  • Michigan resources: our guide to supporting Michigan veterans lists state and national help.

To everyone who has served: thank you. In Michigan, adult-use cannabis is for those 21 and older, and registered patients 18 and older can buy with their card. If you choose to use it, enjoy it responsibly, and don't travel across state lines with cannabis.

Frequently asked questions

Can veterans use medical marijuana?

Yes, under state law, if you qualify. VA doesn't deny care or benefits for taking part in a state program, but its clinicians can't recommend marijuana or complete program forms. In Michigan, PTSD is a listed qualifying condition for a medical card. Federal limits still apply on VA grounds, for VA employees and for anyone still serving.

Will I lose VA benefits for using cannabis?

No, according to VA. Its guidance says veterans won't be denied VA benefits because of marijuana use, and joining a state program doesn't affect eligibility for VA care. VA encourages you to discuss your use with your provider, who will note it in your record. Possession on VA property is still prohibited.

Is PTSD a qualifying condition in Michigan?

Yes. Michigan's medical marijuana program lists PTSD among its qualifying conditions. That makes you eligible to apply; it doesn't mean research has found cannabis effective for PTSD. VA's National Center for PTSD says research so far doesn't support cannabis as an effective PTSD therapy, so talk it through with your clinician.

Can a VA doctor recommend medical marijuana?

No. As of October 2026, VA guidance says its clinicians may not recommend medical marijuana or complete the forms state programs require. They can, and do, discuss cannabis use with you as part of your care. If you apply for a Michigan card, you'll need a certifying physician outside VA.

What is Michigan's veteran marijuana research grant?

It's a grant program from Michigan's marijuana regulator that has funded research on cannabis, veterans' medical conditions and suicide prevention. In 2021, for example, it awarded Wayne State University $7 million for a five-year study of cannabinoids, PTSD and depressive symptoms in veterans. Research like this is built to test questions, not to promise results.
