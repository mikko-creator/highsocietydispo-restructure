Choosing the Right THC Gummies: Dose, Format and Timing

The right gummy starts with the milligrams. Here is how to read the label, compare fast-acting and ratio formats, time a dose to your plans and skip the most common mistakes.

Key takeaways

  • Start with the dose: Michigan caps adult-use gummies at 10 mg of THC per serving and 200 mg per package.
  • Begin at 2.5 mg or less, a quarter of a full-strength 10 mg Michigan gummy, and wait at least 2 hours before taking more.
  • Standard gummies may need up to 2 hours before you feel them, and the effects can stretch from 4 to 12 hours.
  • In a small 2026 study, fast-acting edibles peaked in the blood about 30 minutes sooner, but they were not shown to be shorter-acting.
  • Never mix gummies with alcohol, and never drive after one.

The best THC gummies for you are the ones whose dose, format and timing fit your plans. Start with the milligrams on the label, then choose between standard, fast-acting and ratio gummies, and give yourself plenty of time. Here's how gummies differ from smoking and vaping, how to read a Michigan label, how to time a dose and which mistakes to avoid. Gummies, like all adult-use cannabis, are for adults 21 and older.

How gummies differ from smoking and vaping

When you inhale cannabis, THC reaches your bloodstream through your lungs. Canadian public health officials say inhaled effects can start within seconds to minutes and reach full strength within about 30 minutes. A gummy takes the long way, through your digestive system and liver. The CDC notes that THC in the blood peaks about 1 to 2 hours after eating it, compared with 5 to 10 minutes after smoking, and that the effects last longer when THC is eaten.

That slower, longer arc is the trade-off. In return, gummies give you a measured dose with no smoke, no lingering smell and no guessing about how much you took. For a deeper look at the category, see our guide to what edibles are and how they work.

Why gummy onset varies

The same gummy can take a different amount of time to kick in from one day to the next. A few things can play a part:

  • Food. A full or empty stomach can change how quickly a gummy starts working.
  • Metabolism. Bodies process THC at different speeds.
  • Formulation. Fast-acting gummies use a formula designed to absorb sooner.
  • Tolerance. If you use cannabis often, the same dose may feel weaker.

Because timing varies, the safe plan never changes: wait at least 2 hours before you consider more.

The best THC gummies for you start with the dose

Every licensed Michigan edible lists its THC and CBD content, with a note that actual values can vary by 10%, plus an activation time. Before anything else, work out two numbers:

  • Milligrams per piece. Michigan caps adult-use edibles at 10 mg of THC per serving.
  • Milligrams per package. The cap is 200 mg per package of adult-use edibles. A 100 mg package with 10 pieces holds 10 mg in each.

Canadian public health officials suggest 2.5 mg of THC or less as a first amount for anything you eat or drink. That's a quarter of a full-strength 10 mg gummy, so a lower-dose piece, or a clean cut into quarters, is the place to begin. Products for registered medical patients can hold up to 50 mg per serving, which is far beyond a starting dose. Our THC dosage guide walks through finding a comfortable amount.

Here's how that looks in practice. Say a package holds 200 mg across 20 pieces. Each piece is 10 mg, so a quarter is 2.5 mg and a half is 5 mg. Cut on a clean board with a sharp knife, and wrap the unused pieces back in the original package so the label stays with them.

Vegan or sugar-freePectin instead of gelatin, or sugar alternativesCheck the ingredient list; dosing works the same way

Fast-acting gummies. The 2026 study, led by Colorado State University researchers with 20 participants, found that the fast-acting edible peaked about 30 minutes sooner than a standard one. Peak levels and overall exposure were similar. It was small, and two of its authors worked for companies in the edibles business, so plan cautiously and never take a second piece just because the first hasn't kicked in.

Ratio gummies. Ratios such as 1:1 or 2:1 tell you how much CBD comes with each milligram of THC. Read the milligrams of each, not just the ratio.

CBN gummies. Some gummies add CBN. Research on CBN in people is limited, so judge these by the THC number and your own experience. Our guide to CBN covers what's known so far.

Shapes and packaging. Michigan doesn't allow edibles shaped like people, animals or fruit, so expect simple geometric shapes. Edibles also come in opaque, child-resistant packaging that reseals when it holds more than one serving.

So how do you choose THC gummies from these formats? Pair the format with your plans. A standard gummy suits a planned evening at home, when a slow start doesn't matter. A fast-acting one may fit when you'd rather not wait as long, as long as you still wait before taking more. A ratio gummy suits anyone who wants CBD alongside the THC, and a vegan or sugar-free one simply fits your diet.

Timing a dose to your plans

Gummies reward planning. A few habits make timing easier:

  • Count backward. For a standard gummy, take it about 2 hours before you want to feel it.
  • Plan for the full arc. Effects from eaten cannabis last 4 to 12 hours, and some can last up to 24, so pick a day when you can stay put.
  • Keep your routine steady. Taking a gummy after the same kind of meal each time makes one session easier to compare with the next.
  • Plan the ride. Don't drive after a gummy. Arrange a ride or stay in.

For example, if you'd like to feel a standard gummy by 8 p.m., take it around 6 p.m., after dinner, and plan on staying in for the rest of the night. If 8 p.m. arrives and you feel nothing, give it more time rather than a second piece.

Comparing gummies with cannabis drinks? Our look at THC drinks vs. edibles timing breaks down how the two stack up.

The first and third rows matter most. A slow start tempts people to take more, and a long finish means a dose taken late in the evening may still be working the next morning.

Common mistakes to avoid

  1. Taking more too soon. The CDC has noted that people sometimes eat several servings before the first one kicks in. Wait the full 2 hours.
  2. Starting with a whole 10 mg piece. If you're new, that's four times the suggested starting amount.
  3. Assuming fast-acting means short-acting. In the 2026 study, overall exposure was about the same as with a standard edible.
  4. Adding alcohol. In a 2026 study in JAMA Network Open, adults who ate THC brownies and then drank alcohol showed more driving impairment, and felt more intoxicated, than with either alone.
  5. Storing gummies like snacks. Canadian health officials warn that edible cannabis carries a higher risk of accidental poisoning, especially for children and pets. Keep gummies in their original child-resistant package, locked or up high.
  6. Driving afterward. A gummy can still be working hours after you take it.

For more misconceptions, see our guide to myths about edibles.

Building a gummy rotation

A rotation simply means knowing which products work for which plans:

  • Keep a log. Note the product, the milligrams, the time, what you ate and how it felt.
  • Pick two strengths. A lower-dose gummy for social evenings and a standard one for a quiet night in covers most plans.
  • Change only one variable per session. Switch the product or the dose, not both, so you can tell what made the difference.
  • Never stack products. Trying two different gummies in one session only doubles your dose.

A simple log might look like this:

DayGummy and doseTakenFirst felt itHow it went
FridayStandard, 2.5 mg6 p.m., after dinnerAbout 7:15 p.m.Mild and easy; same dose next time
Next FridayFast-acting, 2.5 mg6 p.m., after dinnerAbout 6:45 p.m.Similar feel, sooner start

After a few entries, you'll know which gummy fits which kind of evening.

If you're pregnant or breastfeeding, take medications or have a health condition, talk with a clinician before trying THC gummies.

Questions to ask a budtender

At any High Society store, our budtenders are happy to go over edible labels with you and help you compare options. Good questions to bring:

  • What's the THC per piece, and for the whole package?
  • Is this a standard or a fast-acting formula?
  • Does it contain CBD or CBN, and in what ratio?
  • What activation time does the label list?
  • Is it vegan or sugar-free?

Frequently asked questions

How many mg should a beginner start with?

No more than 2.5 mg of THC. Canadian public health officials suggest that starting point for anything you eat or drink, and it's a quarter of a full-strength 10 mg Michigan gummy. Wait at least 2 hours before deciding on more, and write down how it felt so you can adjust next time.

Are fast-acting gummies different?

Yes, mainly in timing. They use a formula designed to absorb sooner. In a small 2026 study, THC in the blood peaked about 30 minutes after a fast-acting edible, roughly 30 minutes earlier than with a standard one, while peak levels and overall exposure were similar. Wait before taking more, just as you would with any gummy.

How long do gummies last?

Effects from eaten cannabis usually last 4 to 12 hours, and some can linger up to 24 hours, according to Canadian public health officials. Plan for that long arc: take your gummy early enough that you won't need to drive or be fully alert before it wears off.

What is the strongest gummy sold in Michigan?

For adult use, 10 mg of THC per serving and 200 mg per package. Products for registered medical patients can hold up to 50 mg per serving, still within a 200 mg package. Stronger isn't better for most people: a full-strength 10 mg piece is four times the suggested starting amount.

Are gummies better than vapes?

Neither is better; they're different. Gummies are measured, smoke-free and long-lasting, but slow to start. Vapes act within minutes, and inhaled THC wears off sooner than eaten THC, so it's easier to adjust. Choose by your plans, and never use either before driving.
