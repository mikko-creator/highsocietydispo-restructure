Full Buds, Popcorn Buds or Shake: What You Get for Your Money

Whole buds, smalls and shake often come from the same plant. Here's how they differ in looks, potency, freshness and value, and which one suits joints, bowls or cooking.

Key takeaways

  • One harvest can yield full buds, popcorn buds and shake. Size, handling and freshness set them apart more than the strain does.
  • Popcorn buds are small whole buds, often from lower branches. Many test close to top buds, though the smallest, most shaded ones can test lower.
  • Shake is loose material that breaks off buds. It's cheaper and handy for rolling and cooking, but it loses aroma faster than whole flower.
  • Judge every form by its label: the THC figure, harvest date and test date tell you more than bud size.

Choosing bud vs shake vs popcorn buds comes down to size, handling and freshness more than strain. All three can come from the same plants. Full buds have the most polish and hold their aroma longest. Popcorn buds are smaller and often cost less, and shake, the loose material left behind, is priced lowest and suits rolling or cooking. Here's how each form is made, how potency compares and what to look for at the counter.

Where each form comes from on the plant

A cannabis plant's flowers grow in clusters along its stems. The largest, densest clusters, called colas, form at the top of the main stem and on the upper branches, where light is strongest. Smaller flowers develop lower down and deeper in the canopy. Our guide to cannabis plant anatomy maps every part.

After harvest, buds are dried, cured and trimmed. Along the way, bits of flower, sugar leaf and loose resin break off and collect, and that becomes shake. A 2022 review of postharvest methods notes that good trimming removes most of the sugar leaves while keeping the delicate trichomes, the resin glands that hold most of the cannabinoids, intact.

Full buds: look, potency and uses

Full buds are what most people picture: intact, dense flowers with a heavy coat of trichomes and the full aroma of the strain. Because they're whole, you can judge them by eye and nose before you buy.

Size doesn't promise potency, though. A 2021 study of large medical cannabis plants challenged a common belief in the industry. In most of the growing setups it tested, buds at the tips of side branches, both high and low on the plant, held more of most cannabinoids than the main top cola. The THC figure on the label, which comes from lab testing, is a better guide than looks.

Full buds make sense when you want the complete experience: a bowl, a hand-rolled joint or a dry-herb vaporizer with the aroma intact. Grind them just before use, since broken-up flower loses its scent faster.

Popcorn buds: small nugs from the same plant

Popcorn buds, or smalls, are complete buds that simply stayed small. Many come from lower branches, where less light reaches. They're usually priced lower because they look less impressive, not because they're a different strain.

Potency is often close to that of the top buds, with one exception. In the same 2021 study, cannabinoid levels at most positions on the plant varied little. The smallest buds at the very bottom, where the least light reached, measured up to 40% lower. Popcorn buds from well-lit plants can test close to their bigger siblings, so check the label rather than assuming either way.

Smalls grind and burn like full buds, so they suit everyday bowls and joints. Our guide to popcorn buds covers them in more depth.

Shake vs pre-ground flower: not the same thing

Shake is the loose material that falls off buds during trimming, curing, packing and handling. It can include small flower pieces, sugar leaf and loose trichomes, and its makeup varies from batch to batch. Our explainer on what shake is covers how it's sold and used.

Pre-ground flower is different: whole buds ground on purpose for convenience. It's more uniform than shake, but it shares shake's weak spot. Once flower is broken up, it loses aroma faster.

  • In a 2020 storage study, grinding was among the least favorable conditions for keeping terpenes, the compounds behind aroma.
  • A classic 1976 study found that losses from air exposure could be reduced by keeping the resin glands intact.

Trim is a third, separate category: the sugar leaves cut away during trimming. Sugar leaves hold fewer cannabinoids than the flowers they surround, which is why growers trim them away.

Bud vs shake: how potency compares

Shake can range from nearly as strong as bud to noticeably weaker. Three things drive the difference:

  • What's in it. Trichomes are thickest on the flowers, so flower pieces carry most of the cannabinoids. Extra leaf and stem dilute them.
  • What collected in it. Resin glands can break off during trimming and handling, and some of them settle into shake.
  • How old it is. More exposed surface means faster loss of aroma and cannabinoids over time.

There's no way to eyeball the numbers, so read the label. In Michigan, it gives the THC and CBD figures, names the lab and test date, and adds the harvest date when relevant.

Freshness and quality checks

Whatever form you choose, a quick inspection pays off:

  • Harvest date. Newer usually means fresher aroma.
  • Test date. Shows when the batch was lab tested.
  • Smell. Fresh flower smells distinct. Little or no smell suggests old material, and a musty smell can signal mold.
  • Look. Visible trichomes are a good sign. Mold, webbing, or lots of stems and seeds are not.
  • Feel. Slightly springy buds are well kept, while powdery shake has likely dried out.
  • Color. Green or purple tones with orange hairs are normal. Brown, gray or dusty patches are a warning sign.

Once you're home, store any form airtight in a cool, dark spot. Keep shake and ground flower sealed between uses, since light and air speed up the loss of cannabinoids.

When shake is a smart buy, and what to inspect first

Shake makes sense when:

  • you're cooking or making infused butter or oil
  • you roll often and don't need whole buds
  • you're shopping on a budget and will use it within a few weeks
  • you want to try a strain's flavor before paying for full buds

Skip it when you want a strain's full aroma, or when it will sit in a drawer for months.

Before buying shake, check for a recent harvest date, the THC figure on the label, a fresh smell, and few stems and seeds. Then compare its price per gram with smalls of the same strain. Sometimes popcorn buds cost only a little more and stay fresh longer.

Price per gram, made simple

Divide the price by the number of grams, and compare like with like: the same strain, similar harvest dates and similar THC figures. A larger size often lowers the price per gram, but it only saves money if you'll finish it while it's fresh. Our cannabis budgeting guide has more ways to stretch a budget.

Frequently asked questions

Is shake weaker than bud?

Not always. Because shake breaks off the same buds, it can be nearly as strong, but extra leaf, stems and age can lower its potency. Loose trichomes sometimes collect in shake, too. The THC figure on the label is the only reliable comparison, and a recent harvest date matters even more for shake than for whole buds.

Are popcorn buds lower quality?

Usually not. Popcorn buds are small whole buds, often from lower branches, sold for less because they look less impressive. In one study of large plants, the smallest buds near the base, where the least light reached, tested up to 40% lower, while buds elsewhere varied little. Check the label's THC figure and harvest date, and let your nose weigh in.

What can you do with shake?

Roll it, cook with it or vaporize it. Shake is popular for joints because it's already broken up, and for cannabutter and oils because looks don't matter once it's infused. Pick out stems first, keep it sealed and use it within a few weeks, since broken-up flower loses aroma faster than whole buds.

Why is shake cheaper?

Mostly because of looks and handling. Shake is made of loose pieces that broke off buds during trimming, curing and packing, so it can't be sold as whole flower. It may also hold more leaf and lose aroma sooner. That doesn't make it a bad buy; it simply suits rolling and cooking better than a showpiece bowl.
