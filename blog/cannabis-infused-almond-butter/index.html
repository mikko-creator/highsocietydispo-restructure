Homemade Cannabis-Infused Almond Butter

A homemade nut butter where the infused oil helps the blend turn creamy, and you set the dose per serving from the strength of your own oil.

By the High Society teamPublished October 24, 20263 min read

03min readRecipes
In this article
  1. Dosing: set the dose per serving first
  2. What you need
  3. How to make cannabis almond butter
  4. Storage and labeling
  5. Serving ideas
  6. Recipe
  7. Frequently asked questions

Key takeaways

  • Season and taste the almond butter before the infused oil goes in, never after.
  • Dose per serving × number of servings ÷ your oil's mg per teaspoon = teaspoons of infused oil.
  • Loosen a thick batch with plain oil, never water.
  • It contains tree nuts, and it's a 4-day fridge item; freeze what you won't eat in time.

Cannabis almond butter is a spreadable edible you make in a food processor: toasted almonds blended until creamy, then finished with a measured spoon of canna-oil, which smooths the butter and carries the THC. Peanuts, cashews and other nuts work the same way.

Dosing: set the dose per serving first

Pick a dose per two-tablespoon serving, multiply by the servings and divide by your oil's mg per teaspoon. Two cups of almonds make about 1 cup of butter, or 8 servings. At 2.5 mg each that's 20 mg, which comes to about 1 1/2 teaspoons if your oil's ceiling is roughly 13 mg a teaspoon. Season and taste before the infused oil goes in, since every taste after that is a dose. Our THC dosage guide has more on picking an amount.

What you need

You need almonds, raw or toasted, plus salt and optional maple, cinnamon or vanilla. Add a measured amount of canna-coconut oil or another oil from our canna oil recipe, and keep plain oil handy for thinning. You can order flower for your oil ahead from your local High Society store.

How to make cannabis almond butter

Toast the almonds at 350°F for about 10 minutes and cool them until just warm. Process, scraping often, through crumbs and a thick ball to a smooth paste, 10 to 15 minutes. Season and taste, then stream in the infused oil and blend a full minute. Thin with plain oil, never water, which makes nut butter seize.

Storage and labeling

Label the jar "infused," with the date, the mg per serving and a "contains tree nuts" warning. It's made with homemade infused oil, which the CDC says to refrigerate for no more than 4 days, so keep it in the fridge, use it within 4 days and freeze extra portions. Give it a separate shelf from the household nut butter, one that children and pets can't reach.

Serving ideas

Spread a measured serving on toast or apple slices, stir it into oatmeal or blend it into a cannabis smoothie. If you pair it with infused granola clusters, count both doses. Measure with a tablespoon, since a generous knife-swipe can be double.

Recipe

Makes
About 1 cup (8 two-tablespoon servings)
Prep time
20 minutes
Cook time
10 minutes

Ingredients

  • 2 cups almonds (raw or toasted)
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt, plus more to taste
  • Optional: 1 to 2 teaspoons maple syrup, 1/4 teaspoon cinnamon, 1/4 teaspoon vanilla
  • About 1 1/2 teaspoons canna-coconut oil or another infused oil, measured for your dose (see the note)
  • Plain neutral oil, as needed for thinning

Steps

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F. Toast the almonds on a sheet pan for about 10 minutes, then cool until just warm.

  2. Process the almonds, scraping the bowl often, until they pass from crumbs to a thick ball to a smooth paste, 10 to 15 minutes.

  3. Add the salt and any flavorings, with maple syrup last and sparingly. Taste and adjust now, before the infused oil goes in.

  4. With the motor running, stream in the measured infused oil and process for 1 minute.

  5. If the butter is thick, add plain oil a teaspoon at a time. Never add water.

  6. Transfer to a jar, mark it with the date, the dose per serving and "contains tree nuts," and keep it cold: 4 days in the fridge, or freeze portions.

Dosing note

Estimate only: 8 servings × 2.5 mg = 20 mg, or about 1 1/2 teaspoons of oil whose ceiling is roughly 13 mg per teaspoon. Real oil usually comes in under its ceiling, depending on the starting flower and on decarb and infusion losses. Have one serving, then wait at least 2 hours before deciding on another. Label the jar and keep it somewhere only adults reach.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use peanuts or cashews instead?

Yes. Peanuts, cashews, pecans and hazelnuts all work with the same method, though processing times vary from nut to nut. The dosing math doesn't change: measure the finished batch, count the two-tablespoon servings and size the infused oil to match. Label it with the right allergen.

Why add oil to nut butter?

Two reasons. Nuts release their own oil as they process, but a little extra helps a dry batch turn smooth and spreadable. Here, the infused oil is also what carries the THC, since THC dissolves in fat. Use plain oil for any extra thinning so the dose stays put.

How much infused almond butter is one serving?

Plan on two tablespoons, the serving size this recipe's math uses, and work out its strength from your own batch. In the example above, a serving carries about 2.5 mg at most. Measure with a spoon, try one serving the first time and give it at least 2 hours.
