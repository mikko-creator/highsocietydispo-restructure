The Best Vape Strains, and How to Choose a Cartridge

Which strains vape well, how live resin, distillate, full-spectrum and rosin carts differ, and how to read a vape label before you buy.

By the High Society teamPublished October 24, 20266 min read

06min readCannabis 101

Key takeaways

  • Strains that vape well have strong, distinct terpene profiles, and the extract decides how much of that profile survives in a cart.
  • Live resin and rosin carts keep more of the plant's own terpenes, while distillate carts rely on terpenes added back.
  • Lab-tested examples include Trainwreck and Jack Herer for terpinolene, Gelato for limonene, and Grape Ape and Granddaddy Purple for myrcene.
  • Indica and sativa labels track a few terpenes better than they track genetics, so check the terpene list.
  • Buy licensed, tested vapes only: the 2019 lung-injury outbreak was strongly linked to vitamin E acetate and to THC vapes from informal sources.

The best vape strains are terpene-rich ones whose flavor survives extraction, such as terpinolene-bright Trainwreck for daytime, limonene-forward Gelato for balance and grape-scented Grape Ape for evenings. In a cartridge, though, the extract matters as much as the strain. Here's how to choose both, with classic picks by type and a quick guide to reading a vape label.

What makes a strain vape well

A vape heats cannabis oil or flower without burning it, so the aroma compounds aren't masked by smoke. That's why strains with a strong, distinct terpene profile tend to vape best: a clear citrus, pine, berry or grape note comes through, while a muddy profile can taste flat. In a cartridge, the way the oil was made decides how much of that profile survives. New to vaping? Start with our guide to vaping cannabis.

Cartridge types: live resin, distillate, full-spectrum and rosin

TypeHow it's madeFlavor
Live resinExtracted from flash-frozen plants using solventsClosest to the fresh plant's aroma
DistillateTHC refined into a high-potency oil, with terpenes added backDepends on the added terpenes
Full-spectrumAn extract that keeps a broad range of cannabinoids and terpenesRich and layered; the term isn't standardized
RosinPressed from flower or hash with heat, no solventsRich and natural

Live resin cartridges suit flavor-first shoppers, since freezing the plants at harvest helps hold on to more of their terpenes. Distillate carts are consistent and potent, and their terpenes may come from cannabis or from other plants; on a distillate cart, the strain name may describe the flavor blend that was added back. Rosin carts skip solvents entirely. Our guide to live resin goes deeper.

Disposables, 510 carts and pods

  • 510-thread cartridges screw onto a standard reusable battery, so you can switch strains easily.
  • Disposables combine the battery and the oil in one device, ready to use and replaced when empty. Check local rules for recycling the battery. A full spectrum disposable vape combines rich flavor with no setup at all.
  • Pods click into a matching battery from the same maker and don't fit other systems.

Our guides to disposable vapes and vape pens cover the hardware.

Best vape strains for evenings: indica-leaning picks

These strains are usually sold as indica or indica-leaning, with earthy, sweet or grape-heavy profiles that carry well into a cart:

  • Grape Ape. A grape-and-berry strain usually sold as indica. In one lab's testing of flower from a California dispensary, it belonged to a myrcene-dominant purple group.
  • Granddaddy Purple. Another member of that purple group, with ripe grape and berry notes.
  • Master Kush. An earthy, hash-like classic often listed as indica. The same study found it led by limonene, beta-caryophyllene and myrcene, with more alpha-bisabolol than most cultivars tested.

If you shop for an indica vape cartridge and want that grape or hash character to come through, choose live resin or rosin over distillate.

Sativa-leaning picks for daytime

Sativa-labeled strains often lean bright and herbal. A few with lab-tested profiles:

  • Trainwreck. Lemon and pine over musky earth, usually sold as sativa-leaning, and terpinolene-dominant in testing.
  • Jack Herer. Pine, herbs and citrus, and terpinolene-dominant in the same study.
  • Strawberry Cough. Sweet strawberry with floral and herbal notes, reportedly sativa-dominant. Lab testing put it in a myrcene-led group with more ocimene than limonene.

If you like a bright, herbal flavor in a sativa vape pen, look for terpinolene near the top of the terpene list.

Hybrid vape strains for balance

  • Gelato. Sweet cream, citrus and berry. It tested limonene-led and tended to carry the most linalool of any cultivar in the study.
  • OG Kush. Lemon, pine and a fuel-like edge, with limonene and myrcene in roughly equal parts.
  • Blue Dream. Sweet berry with a piney lift, myrcene-dominant with extra alpha- and beta-pinene.

Hybrid vape strains are a good middle ground if you want flavor without committing to a strongly sativa- or indica-labeled profile. Keep in mind that research has found these labels track a few terpenes better than they track genetics, so the terpene list is the more useful guide.

Reading a vape label: total THC, terpene source, extraction method

Michigan requires every cannabis label to show THC and CBD content, with a note that actual values may vary by 10%. It also requires the producer, the testing lab and test date, and the harvest date where it applies. Three more details are worth looking for, even though they aren't required:

  • Extraction method, such as live resin, rosin or distillate.
  • Terpene source. Cannabis-derived terpenes come from the plant, while botanical terpenes come from other plants.
  • Terpene list, ideally with the top two or three named.

When you're choosing the best strains for vape carts, these details show whether you're getting the strain's own profile or a recreation of it. Our THC vape buying guide covers potency and hardware in more depth.

Buy licensed, tested vapes only

In 2019 and 2020, a national outbreak of serious lung injuries linked to vaping led to 2,807 reported hospitalized cases or deaths by February 2020. The CDC found that vitamin E acetate, an additive used in some THC vaping products, was strongly linked to the outbreak. It also found that THC products from informal sources, such as friends, family or dealers, were linked to most cases. At the time, the CDC and FDA advised people not to use THC vaping products, especially from informal sources, and not to add anything to a vape.

Licensed Michigan products are tested, and the label names the testing lab and test date. At High Society, we work with trusted vendors whose products have passed Michigan's testing standards. Buy only from licensed stores, use the battery a cart was made for, and never refill or modify a cartridge.

Frequently asked questions

What strain is best for a vape cart?

The one whose aroma you already enjoy, in an extract that keeps it. Terpene-forward strains such as Jack Herer, Gelato or Granddaddy Purple carry over well when the oil is live resin or rosin. With distillate, flavor comes from terpenes added back, so the strain name says more about taste than about the plant's chemistry.

Is live resin better than distillate?

For flavor, usually; for strength, not necessarily. Live resin is extracted from flash-frozen plants to keep more of their original terpenes, so it aims to taste closer to the strain. Distillate is a refined, high-THC oil with terpenes added back, which keeps it consistent from cart to cart. Choose based on what you value most.

Are disposable vapes worth it?

They can be, for convenience. A disposable needs no separate battery or setup, which suits occasional use. A 510 battery with replaceable carts creates less waste over time and lets you switch strains easily. Either way, buy licensed, tested products from a licensed store, and never refill or modify a disposable.

Why do some vape carts taste better than others?

Mostly because of terpenes and extraction. Live resin and rosin keep more of the plant's own aroma, while distillate carts depend on the terpenes added back, which may come from cannabis or from other plants. Fresh oil, clean hardware and a lower temperature setting, if your battery has one, help the flavor too.
