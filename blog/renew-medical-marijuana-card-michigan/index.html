Renewing Your Michigan Medical Marijuana Card: Steps, Timing and Costs

Your Michigan registry card is good for two years. Here's when to start renewing, what the physician certification and $40 fee involve, and what changes if the card lapses.

By the High Society teamPublished October 24, 20264 min read

04min readResources
In this article

  2. When to renew your Michigan medical marijuana card
  3. Step 1: get a new physician certification
  4. Step 2: apply through the state's online registry
  5. Fees, processing time and proof while you wait
  6. If your card expires: shopping adult-use at 21+
  7. Shopping as a medical patient at High Society
  8. Frequently asked questions

Key takeaways

  • A Michigan registry card is valid for two years; you can renew starting 90 days before it expires.
  • Each renewal needs a new physician certification, and patients without a caregiver can apply online.
  • The state fee is $40, and issuing a card can take up to 20 business days plus mail time.
  • If your card lapses, you shop as an adult-use customer, which means being 21 or older.

To renew a Michigan medical marijuana card, you get a new physician certification and send a renewal application to the state's medical marijuana registry, run by the Cannabis Regulatory Agency (CRA). You can apply up to 90 days before your card expires, and patients without a caregiver can submit the application online. Here's the order to do it in, and what changes if your card lapses.

Why renewing on time matters

A valid card is what makes your purchases medical ones:

  • Age. Patients 18 to 20 can buy only with a valid card. Without one, dispensary shopping is for adults 21 and older.
  • Taxes. Michigan doesn't charge its 10% adult-use excise tax on medical sales to registered patients. The 6% sales tax still applies.
  • Protection. Michigan's medical law protects you from arrest only when you can show a valid registry card and photo ID. An application or physician form doesn't count as a card.

For more reasons patients keep their cards current, see our look at Michigan medical card benefits.

When to renew your Michigan medical marijuana card

A Michigan patient registry card is valid for two years. The state accepts renewal applications starting 90 days before the expiration date on your card. Because issuing a card can take up to 20 business days plus mail time, book your physician visit about three months before the card expires.

Step 1: get a new physician certification

Each renewal needs a new physician certification. The CRA's instructions call for a medical evaluation by an active Michigan physician within the last six months, and its guidance describes that evaluation as in person. If you need to find a certifying physician, our guide on getting certified in Michigan explains your options.

Step 2: apply through the state's online registry

Patients without a caregiver can renew through the CRA's secure online portal, and so can patients who are removing their caregiver at renewal. If you have a caregiver or want to name one, you'll use the paper application packet instead. For first-time applicants, our Michigan medical card guide covers the full process.

Fees, processing time and proof while you wait

As of October 2026, the state's fee is $40, and the CRA's online portal takes payment by credit card or electronic check.

The program must approve or deny an application within 15 business days of receiving it, and it has up to 20 business days to issue a card or a denial letter. Mail adds more time. Online applicants may receive a temporary registry card by email in the meantime.

If your old card expires before the new card or a temporary card arrives and you're 21 or older, you'll shop as an adult-use customer until it does.

If your card expires: shopping adult-use at 21+

An expired card works like no card. If you're 21 or older, you can still shop adult-use with a valid government-issued ID or passport, but those purchases carry the 10% excise tax. If you're 18 to 20, you'll need to wait until your renewal comes through. Our guide to buying medical vs. recreational cannabis compares the two.

Shopping as a medical patient at High Society

High Society welcomes medical patients 18 and older with a valid medical marijuana card. Bring your registry card and a valid photo ID, order ahead for in-store or curbside pickup, and find your nearest store. If you haven't yet, Join The Society, our free loyalty program, to earn points on qualifying purchases.

Frequently asked questions

How long is a Michigan medical marijuana card valid?

Two years. You can submit a renewal application starting 90 days before the expiration date on your card. Processing can take up to 20 business days plus mailing time, so see your physician and apply early in that window, before your current card runs out.

How much does it cost to renew a Michigan medical card?

As of October 2026, the state charges $40, the same as a first-time application, and the CRA's online portal accepts credit cards and electronic checks. Your physician's evaluation is a separate cost, so ask about it when you book the appointment.

Can I renew my Michigan medical card online?

Yes, if you don't have a caregiver. Patients without one, including those removing a caregiver at renewal, can renew and pay through the CRA's online portal. If you have a caregiver or want to add one, you'll mail the paper application packet instead.

What happens if my medical card expires?

Your purchases become adult-use purchases. If you're 21 or older, you can still shop with a valid photo ID, but you'll pay the 10% excise tax. If you're 18 to 20, you can't buy until your renewal comes through. An application or physician form doesn't count as a card.
