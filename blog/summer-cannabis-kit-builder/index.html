Your Summer Cannabis Lineup, One Format at a Time

Lake days, cookouts and up-north weekends call for a lineup that travels well. Build yours one format at a time, then check Michigan's summer rules before you go.

Key takeaways

  • Build around a format you already enjoy, add one easy option and, if you're experienced, one upgrade.
  • Pre-rolls and vapes travel easily; edibles need a cool, shaded spot to survive summer heat.
  • Michigan caps an adult-use edible serving at 10 mg of THC, as of October 2026, and eaten THC peaks about 1.5 to 3 hours after you take it.
  • Keep consumption on private property: no public beaches or parks, and no use while driving a car or a boat.
  • Federal land such as Sleeping Bear Dunes falls under federal law, so leave cannabis at home for those trips.

A good summer cannabis lineup is small, portable and built around what you already enjoy. Start with your usual format, add one easy option for last-minute plans, and add an upgrade only if you're experienced. Pack everything sealed and keep edibles cool. Then keep consumption on private property, because Michigan doesn't allow it in public places like beaches and parks, or for anyone driving a car or a boat.

Start from what you already like

Summer is a fine time to try something new, but a lake weekend three hours from home isn't the place for a big experiment. Build around the format you know, and think of anything new as a small add-on.

A few questions help:

  • How do you usually consume, and how much?
  • Where will you be? A backyard, a friend's cottage and a rented cabin each come with their own rules.
  • Who's coming? Everyone partaking needs to be 21 or older.

Our budtenders can help you build around those answers at any of our eight Michigan stores. You can also browse your store's menu on our shop page and order ahead for in-store or curbside pickup.

Flower for slow evenings, and how to judge freshness

Flower is the classic pick for an evening on a private porch or around a backyard campfire. Freshness is easy to check:

  • Harvest date. Michigan labels list it, so you can see how recently the flower was grown.
  • Look. Buds should look intact, not dusty or crumbled.
  • Aroma. A strong, clean smell is a good sign. Flat or musty is not.

Heat is flower's summer enemy. Keep it in an airtight jar, out of the sun and out of hot cars. Our guide to storing cannabis covers containers, light and humidity.

Pre-rolls for last-minute plans

When a friend texts about a bonfire tonight, pre-rolls are the easy answer. They're ready to go, come in singles and multi-packs, and need nothing but a lighter.

Carry them in a crush-proof tube or their original packaging so they don't get bent or damp in a backpack or cooler bag. Mini pre-rolls are handy when you want a shorter session, and our pre-roll buyer's guide compares sizes, infused options and what to look for.

Vapes for portable, low-smell use

Vapes are compact and leave less lingering smell than smoke, which suits a cottage weekend where not everyone partakes.

Two summer cautions. Heat can thin vape oil, so don't leave a cartridge in a hot car or in direct sun. And low smell doesn't change the rules: vaping is still consumption, so the same limits on public use apply.

Buy cartridges from a licensed store, where products are tested. Our THC vape buying guide covers hardware, oil types and labels.

Edibles for no-smoke afternoons: serving sizes, heat and storage

Edibles skip smoke and smell entirely, which makes them a natural fit for a long afternoon on private property. They also take their time. In a controlled study, effects from eaten THC took 30 to 60 minutes to appear and peaked around 1.5 to 3 hours. Start low and give it time before you consider more.

In Michigan, an adult-use edible serving tops out at 10 mg of THC, with up to 200 mg in a package, as of October 2026. Cannabis drinks follow the same 10 mg serving limit, with up to 100 mg in a container. Look at the label's serving size before you pack.

Summer heat is hard on edibles:

  • Chocolate melts and gummies stick together in a hot car or a sunny window.
  • A small insulated bag or a cooler with an ice pack keeps them firm.
  • Keep them in their original child-resistant packaging, in a separate, clearly marked container away from regular snacks.
  • Never leave them where kids or pets can reach them.

That last point matters most at busy summer gatherings, where a gummy can look like any other snack.

Concentrates as the experienced upgrade

Concentrates such as live resin and live rosin are far stronger than flower and prized for their flavor. They also need gear, like a dab rig or a concentrate vaporizer.

That makes them an upgrade for experienced adults, not a vacation experiment. Keep them in a fridge or a cool bag, since heat changes their texture quickly, and start with a small amount if you're away from your usual setup.

The three-slot formula for a summer cannabis kit

Here's a simple way to build a lineup without overbuying:

  1. The base. The format you already know and like. This is most of your kit.
  2. The easy add-on. Something for spontaneous moments, like a pre-roll pack or a box of low-dose edibles.
  3. The upgrade. One new thing to try at a calm moment: a new strain, a cannabis drink or, for experienced adults, a concentrate.

Michigan summer rules: where you can and can't consume

Michigan lets adults 21 and older carry up to 2.5 ounces of cannabis, with no more than 15 grams of it as concentrate. Where you consume is another matter. As of October 2026:

  • No public consumption. Using cannabis in a public place isn't allowed, and public beaches and parks are public places.
  • Private property, with permission. Owners can ban cannabis use or smoking on their property, so check the rules at a rented cottage before you light up.
  • Not while driving or boating. No one may use cannabis while driving or in control of a car, motorboat, snowmobile or off-road vehicle. Smoking isn't allowed in a car's passenger area on a public road, and driving while impaired is never covered.
  • Travel sealed. Transporting cannabis within the limit is allowed. Keeping it sealed in its packaging and in the trunk is the cautious habit.
  • Federal land is different. Michigan's law doesn't protect you in places like Sleeping Bear Dunes or the national forests, where federal law applies. Leave cannabis at home for those trips.
  • Stay in Michigan. Don't travel across state lines with cannabis. Bringing a Michigan purchase into Indiana is illegal under Indiana law, so if you shop at our New Buffalo store near the state line, enjoy it on the Michigan side.

Frequently asked questions

What are the must-haves for a summer cannabis lineup?

A base format you know, one easy add-on for spontaneous plans, and a way to keep everything sealed and cool. For most people that means a favorite flower or vape, a pre-roll pack or low-dose edibles, an airtight container and a small cooler. Add a new product only if you'll try it somewhere calm, with nowhere to drive afterward.

Which formats travel best for a day out?

Pre-rolls, vapes and edibles are the most portable, as long as they stay sealed and out of the heat. Keep them in their original packaging, pack edibles in an insulated bag, and keep everything in the trunk while you drive. Portable doesn't mean public, though: save consumption for private property where you have permission.

Can I use cannabis at a Michigan beach or state park?

No. Michigan law doesn't allow cannabis use in public places, and public beaches and state parks are public places. National lands such as Sleeping Bear Dunes and the national forests are federal ground, where Michigan's law offers no protection. Plan to enjoy your purchase on private property instead; our Michigan cannabis law guide covers the details.

Is cannabis allowed on a boat in Michigan?

Not for the person running it, and it's wise to keep the whole boat cannabis-free. Michigan law bars using cannabis while operating or in control of a motorboat, and operating one while impaired isn't allowed either. Summer lakes are busy, and a boat is no place to take chances. Save it for later on private property, once everyone is safely back on shore.

How do I keep gummies from melting in summer?

Keep them cool and shaded. An insulated lunch bag or a small cooler with an ice pack works well; a parked car or a sunny dashboard doesn't. Store them in their original child-resistant package inside the cooler, clearly apart from regular snacks. If gummies melt together, portions get hard to judge, so cut carefully and go smaller.
