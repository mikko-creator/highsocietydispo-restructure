Live Resin or Live Rosin? How They're Made and Which to Choose

Both start with fresh-frozen cannabis. One is extracted with a solvent, the other with ice water and a heat press. Here's how they compare and how to pick.

By the High Society teamPublished October 24, 20266 min read

06min readResources

Key takeaways

  • "Live" means the plants were frozen right after harvest instead of dried and cured, to keep more of their terpenes.
  • Live resin is extracted with a hydrocarbon solvent that is purged afterward; live rosin uses only ice water, heat and pressure.
  • Rosin is often priced higher, since separating resin without solvents yields less oil and takes more hands-on work.
  • Both are strong concentrates, so start with a very small amount and check the THC figure on the label.

Live resin vs. live rosin comes down to how the oil leaves the plant. Both begin with cannabis that was frozen the moment it was harvested. Live resin is then extracted with a solvent, while live rosin is pressed from ice-water hash using nothing but heat and pressure. That one step shapes the texture, the price and, for many people, the flavor.

Why terpenes are the point of "live" concentrates

Terpenes are the aromatic compounds behind every strain's smell, from lemon peel to pine to fuel. Researchers credit terpenes, not cannabinoids, with the aroma of cannabis, and both are made in the plant's tiny resin glands, called trichomes. Our guide to cannabis terpenes covers them in depth.

Many terpenes are volatile. The lighter ones fade as cannabis dries and sits in storage, so cured flower rarely smells as loud as a living plant. Live concentrates are built to catch more of that fresh aroma.

What "live" means: fresh-frozen cannabis

Most flower is dried for a week or two, then cured before it's packaged. Live products skip that stage: the plants are frozen fresh, without drying, and kept frozen until extraction day.

Cold storage slows the loss of volatile compounds such as terpenes, and the cold also makes the resin glands brittle, which matters to rosin makers. The trade calls this starting material fresh frozen, and both products begin there.

How live resin is made

Live resin is a solvent extract. At a licensed processor, technicians wash the frozen plant material with a chilled hydrocarbon solvent, usually butane or propane, inside closed equipment. The solvent dissolves the cannabinoids and aroma compounds and leaves most of the plant matter behind.

Next comes the purge, which removes the solvent under mild conditions. Then the extract is worked into its final form: controlled heating and crystallization can separate THCA crystals, called diamonds, from a terpene-rich sauce, and other batches become sugar or badder. Our overview of cannabis extraction methods explains how these techniques compare.

Hydrocarbon extraction has become the dominant way to make non-distilled concentrates for dabbing and vaping. Licensed live resin sold in Michigan has also passed state-required lab testing before it reaches a dispensary shelf.

How live rosin is made

Live rosin is solventless. Nothing goes into the process except water, ice, heat and pressure, and it happens in two stages.

  1. Wash. The frozen plants are stirred gently in ice water. The cold makes the resin glands brittle, so they break away and can be separated from the softer plant material. What's collected is ice-water hash, which has to be dried before it's pressed.
  2. Press. The dried hash is pressed between heated plates, and the heat and pressure squeeze out the oil. Some makers then let it rest in cool storage, which changes its texture to a badder or jam.

For the full process, read our solventless live rosin explainer.

Live resin vs. live rosin: key differences

Live resinLive rosin
Starting materialFresh-frozen flowerFresh-frozen flower, washed into ice-water hash
How it's extractedHydrocarbon solvent, then purgedIce water, then heat and pressure
Common texturesSauce, sugar, badder, diamondsBadder, jam, sauce-like rosin
FlavorBright and terpene-forwardTerpene-forward; fans say it tastes closest to the plant
Yield per batchHigherLower
PriceVaries by productOften priced higher

Flavor is personal. Some people taste little difference, while others prefer rosin's clean, hash-like finish. Potency overlaps too: both are concentrates, so compare the THC figures on the labels instead of assuming one is stronger.

Price is the clearest gap. Separating resin without solvents yields less oil and takes more hands-on work, so rosin is often priced higher. For today's prices, check your store's live menu.

What they have in common

  • Both start with fresh-frozen cannabis rather than dried and cured flower.
  • Both aim to keep as much of the plant's terpene profile as possible.
  • Both are potent, so a serving is tiny compared with flower.
  • Both can be dabbed, and both turn up in vape cartridges.
  • Licensed versions of both pass Michigan's required testing before they're sold.

They sit alongside shatter, wax, kief and the rest of the family covered in our guide to cannabis concentrates.

Carts or dabs? Formats for each

Dabs. Both come in small jars for a dab rig, an electronic rig or a dab pen. A lower temperature keeps more of the flavor, since heat speeds the loss of terpenes. Start with the smallest portion you can manage, or add a little to a bowl of flower instead.

Vape carts. Live resin is widely used in vape cartridges. Live rosin carts and pods exist too, so check what your store carries. When shoppers weigh live rosin vs. live resin carts, price and availability often decide it.

Other uses. See our look at how people use live resin for more ways to enjoy it.

Which one should you choose?

Choose live resin if you want rich flavor, often at a friendlier price, or if you mostly use vape carts. Choose live rosin if you'd rather have a product made without solvents and don't mind paying more. If you're new to concentrates, either one is a big step up from flower, so take a small amount and wait before having more.

Frequently asked questions

Is live rosin better than live resin?

Not across the board. Rosin is solventless, and many enthusiasts prize its flavor. Live resin also carries a rich terpene profile, often at a lower price, and it's widely used in vape carts. The better pick depends on your budget, how you like to consume and whether a solventless process matters to you.

Why is live rosin so expensive?

Mostly yield and labor. Separating resin glands with ice water instead of a solvent tends to yield less, and pressing that hash returns only part of its weight as oil. The process is also hands-on, so rosin is often priced higher than solvent extracts. Check your store's live menu for current prices.

Is live resin solventless?

No. Live resin is made with a solvent, usually a hydrocarbon such as butane or propane, which is purged out before the product is finished. Licensed products sold in Michigan pass state-required lab testing, and the label names the lab and the test date. If you want a concentrate made without solvents, look for rosin instead.

Are live resin carts safe?

Buy them only from a licensed dispensary. Licensed carts in Michigan are lab tested, while the 2019 vaping lung-injury outbreak was tied mainly to THC vapes from informal sources and to vitamin E acetate. Any inhaled product carries some risk, so go easy, never modify a cart, and talk to your doctor if you have heart or lung concerns.
